The truce of the Peace of Ramaḍān ran out on 25 Ṣafar 813, and the Emperor [Ismāʿīl](person:ismail-ibn-saad) declared war on Qashtāla the same day. It was the fourth war of the Istirdād, and the Emperor named it himself, in letters the Dīwān al-Inshāʾ carried to every court from Fās to Paris. He did not call it a war for towns. He called it the War of Jillīqiya, by the name the first Arabs had given to the whole Christian country beyond the Duero. Mūsā ibn Nuṣayr had ridden into Jillīqiya in the first years of al-Andalus, he wrote, and al-Manṣūr had marched across it to Shant Yāqub and carried the bells of its church back to Qurṭuba to hang as lamps in the Great Mosque. What they had begun, the house of Naṣr would finish. The ʿulamāʾ read the letters aloud in the mosques. Portugal stood with Qashtāla, as it always had. The sultan [Abū al-Faḍl](person:abu-al-fadl-marinid) of [Fās](place:fas) sent his armies and his fleet, with the men of Tilimsān and Tafilalt. The Mamlūks were busy putting the Fertile Crescent in order, and the Emperor did not ask them to leave it.

The war began badly. Off Cape Mondego the Castilian admiral Pedro, a seaman of real skill, sent the whole fleet of Tilimsān to the bottom without losing a ship. At Peñafiel the little army of [Qūnka](place:qunka) threw itself on the Castilian host and was destroyed. And in Shaʿbān the Castilian general Crisanto, a careful man who never fought on ground he had not chosen, broke the Catalan army at Cuéllar, south of the Duero.

The Emperor had never been taken for a soldier, and he knew it. He was a man who set himself one aim and gave every order to it, and his aim that winter was Crisanto. He took the army north himself and found the Castilians on the same field at Cuéllar. He attacked on 17 Ramaḍān 813, the anniversary of Badr, with twenty-five thousand Andalusīs and Marīnids against nineteen thousand. It was the hardest day of his life. Nearly ten thousand of ours fell, and more than eight thousand of theirs. At nightfall the field was his, and the preachers of [Ishbīliya](place:ishbiliyya) had their sermon for the next Friday.

The sea turned next. In Dhū al-Qaʿda the admiral Pedro tried his luck once more, with fourteen ships against the joined fleets of al-Andalus and Fās, a hundred and seven sail under the Marīnid prince Aḥmad, in the bay of Setúbal below [al-Ushbūna](place:al-ushbuna). None of the fourteen came home. In Muḥarram 814 the Emperor met Crisanto a second time, at Toro outside Zamora, and broke him again. In Ṣafar the Portuguese fleet came out against thirty-four ships at the mouth of the Duero: eleven of Balansiyya under their own admiral, and twenty-three of al-Andalus under the crown prince [Muḥammad](person:muhammad-ibn-ismail-ibn-saad), in his first command. Fifteen Portuguese ships were sunk or taken, for three of ours. The prince had never shone in the dīwān, but on a deck he was another man, and he would talk of ships for the rest of his life. In Jumādā al-Ūlā the Emperor caught the Portuguese army at Besteiros and destroyed more than half of it.

On 13 Jumādā al-Ūlā 814 Qashtāla surrendered without conditions. Its armies were gone, and the Emperor's flags flew from Zamora to Shant Yāqub, as al-Manṣūr's had four hundred years before. Portugal fought on. The dīwān told the Emperor he could have a separate peace with it whenever he wished, and a province for the asking. He refused. He had not come for a province, and this war would be the end of Portugal. He marched on Porto, its capital, and sent the crown prince round with the fleet to close the river mouth behind it. Porto held out for a month. Then the grain ran out. On 18 Jumādā al-Ākhira the starving city rose against its own garrison, and the townsmen threw open the gates to the army.

The nobles of Portugal had one army left. In Rajab it came down from the hills to fall on a camp of Marīnid levies, and the Emperor caught it on the march at Guimarães, where their first king had been born. On 9 Rajab he fell on it with twice its number and broke it, and Portugal had no army left.

Five days later, on 14 Rajab 814, the peace was signed. Rajab is one of the sacred months, in which the Prophet forbade war, and the court called it the Peace of Rajab and said it was fitting that this war should end in it. Portugal ceased to be. Its thirty-four towns, from Porto and Braga to Coimbra and Viseu, passed to the crown, and its king [Fernando](person:fernando-of-castile) went into exile at the court of the man who had dragged him into the war. Qashtāla gave up sixty-three towns, the whole of Old Castile, León and Navarre, from Valladolid and Burgos to Segovia, Soria and Pamplona. King [Pedro](person:pedro-of-castile) kept forty-five towns in the rain of the north, in Galicia, Asturias and the Basque hills, and moved his court to Oviedo. The Christians had always said their reconquest began in those mountains, when a Gothic lord held a cave against the first governors of al-Andalus. Seven hundred years later they had been driven back to the same mountains.

On the day of the peace the crown ruled two hundred and fifty-eight towns and nearly four million souls, three of every four of them Christians. It did not keep them long. The Emperor had learned from his father that Christians obey a lord who owes his crown to the Emperor more readily than a governor, and he gave out the conquests at once as four new ṭāʾifas. His cousin [Aḥmad](person:ahmad-ibn-ismail-ibn-yusuf) took the old kingdom of Portugal as the Ṭāʾifa of Burtuqāl, after the old name of Porto, Portucale, which the Arabs said as Burtuqāl and from which the Christians had named their kingdom. His own son [Aḥmad](person:ahmad-ibn-ismail-ibn-saad), eighteen, took León. His cousin [Yūsuf](person:yusuf-ibn-ismail-ibn-yusuf) took Navarre, with [Banbalūna](place:banbaluna). And Old Castile, from Burgos to Valladolid and Segovia, went to [Khālid ibn Yaḥyā Raḥḥū](person:khalid-ibn-yahya), sixty-eight years old, the general of the Marīnid war, the captain of the prospectors and the victor of Granadilla, the first man outside the house of Naṣr to be given a ṭāʾifa. Europe was horrified, all but Paris, and the Emperor of the Germans could do nothing while France and England were at war again. The ʿulamāʾ said the Istirdād was not finished. Qashtāla still held the mountains of the north, and one more war would push it into the sea.

*When the victory of Allāh has come and the conquest, and you see the people entering into the religion of Allāh in multitudes, then exalt with praise of your Lord and ask forgiveness of Him.*
