This note covers three entries: the Jamāʿa of the commons, Sardinia and Bastia, and the landing at Syracuse.

**What the saves show.** There are two autosaves (1 February 1448 and 1 February 1449) and a save of 2 August 1449.

*The Jamāʿa.* The privilege "peasants represented in parliament" first appears in the 1449 autosave. The commons' satisfaction is 50 before it, 59 in February 1449 and 54 in August. The burghers go from 54 to 56. The Jamāʿa also gained an urban seat at Lisbon. Its date, that the two princes called it, and the uproar come from Trent's account.

*The war at sea and on land.* The October–December 1447 battles, the failed allied attacks on Palermo (four, September 1448 to April 1449), Gabès's separate peace (1 December 1448) and Corsica and Sardinia under occupation in August 1449 are all from the saves.

*Yūsuf's battles.* The save names Yūsuf as commander at Terracina (5 January 1448), Termini (13 January 1448) and the Corsica Channel (21 October 1448). The figures for Seddori and the Corsica Channel come from Trent's battle screens.

*Who commanded at Seddori.* The save lists the stack's commander as Peter, Banbalūna's general, whose men fought there too. Trent's account says the army was under Abū al-Ḥasan ʿAlī. The entry gives it to Abū al-Ḥasan ʿAlī ibn Aḥmad, the realm's general since 1429 and the victor of Santiago in 1430.

*The ṭāʾifas.* Three changes of ruler:
- The king of Portugal, Abū al-Ḥasan ʿAlī, died at sea on 1 June 1448. His widow Christine bore a daughter the next day and is regent until 1456 for their daughter Fāṭima, born 1440.
- Maryam of Qūnka died on 28 June 1448 and her son succeeded her. The 1449 save marks him an entrepreneur.
- Aḥmad of Banbalūna died of camp fever on 18 February 1449 and his son succeeded him. The save gives no traits for the son; "gentler" is invented.

*The Mamlūk war.* The war on the Banū Lām (the game's "1st Egypt–Lam War") began on 5 November 1448 for al-Ghazāla and al-Rawḍa. Al-Andalus is in it but has fought no battle. The thirteen battles to August 1449 and the occupations come from the save. Trent's account is the source for the Mamlūks' trouble on the Red Sea coast.

*Hungary.* Bohemia's war for the Hungarian throne began on 7 September 1447. By August 1449 Bohemia held 94 Hungarian locations and Poland 14. Naples fights on Hungary's side.

*The army.* Bastia's fall (17 September 1449, after 265 days), the 4,000 Tagri raised in late October 1449, and the 8,700 professional men landed at Syracuse on 15 February 1450 all come from Trent's account. There is no save yet past August 1449.

*Other changes.* The fleet grew from 9 war galleys to 22, with 10 hulks, during 1448. The palace at Seville shows as complete in the 1449 save. Stability rose from 10.7 to 19.3, and political influence reached 100.

*Palermo.* Islamic rule in Palermo (831–1072) is real history.

**Invented colour.**

*Jamāʿa entry:*
- Christine keeping the condolences and not the advisers.
- Abū al-Ḥasan ʿAlī ibn Saʿd liked by Qūnka's merchants.
- The month the Jamāʿa was called, and its ordinary business.
- The Emperor coming in himself.
- The umarāʾ's shouting, the ʿulamāʾ walking out, and the burghers' silence.
- Aḥmad's silence and its reason.
- Ismāʿīl's work along the benches, and the seating compromise.

*Bastia entry:*
- The army's march inland.
- The sailors' "lucky".
- The Emperor answering Cairo as always.
- The Banbalūna men's verdict on the son.
- The general hearing of Āram's birth in the trenches.
- The keys of Bastia hung in the palace.

*Syracuse entry:*
- The sultan asking for men.
- The princes' division of labour: Ismāʿīl argues, Aḥmad reckons.
- The Emperor's indifference to the army.
- The Tagri as sons of the old frontier.
- The muster-masters, and the captains' saying.
- The court reading the Hungarian war as good timing.
