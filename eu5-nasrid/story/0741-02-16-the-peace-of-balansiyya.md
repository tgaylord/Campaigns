On the first of Ṣafar in the year 741 [Jumalla](place:jumalla) fell to the main army, the same town where the war had first drawn blood a year before. The host had won every battle it fought, but it was starving. Each day the quartermasters found less to put in the pot, and the road home was still closed at Mursiyya.

But the enemy did not know it. The army of Arghūn and its allies had dwindled to some six thousand men, and ours stood near sixteen thousand: the Volunteers at Alsh, Ibn al-Khaṭīb's host at Jumalla. So envoys went north to the Aragonese king and told him plainly that unless he made peace, the army of Gharnāṭa would march up the coast and burn Barshalūna to the ground. In truth the host would hardly have passed the fortress of [Shāṭiba](place:shatiba). Had the king known it, the war would have ended in ruin instead of glory. He did not know it. God veiled our hunger from his eyes and showed him only our banners.

On the sixteenth of Ṣafar 741 the peace was sealed. Arghūn gave up everything south of al-Qasṭalyūn and Tīrwāl. That meant [Balansiyya](place:balansiyya) the great city, lost to the Christians a hundred and two years before, and Shāṭiba of the fortress, [Dāniya](place:daniya) and [Qandiya](place:qandiya) on the coast, [al-Qant](place:al-qant) and [Alsh](place:alsh), [al-Qūy](place:al-quy), [Ayūra](place:ayura), [Bunyūl](place:bunyul), [Shalba](place:shalba) and [Līriya](place:liriya), together with [Uryūla](place:uryula) and Jumalla, where our men had bled. He agreed as well to pay reparations for ten years, and the treasury, which had held a hundred and twenty dīnārs in Dhū al-Ḥijja, held six hundred and seventy by the day of the peace.

So the realm that had been eighteen towns grew to thirty-one, and its souls from four hundred and eighty-five thousand to six hundred and eighty-three thousand. Among them now are a hundred and forty-seven thousand Christians, Catalans and Aragonese and Castilians, who must be ruled justly if they are to be ruled at all. Three days after the peace the sultan paid the hired companies their wages early and sent them away, and put the host back on peacetime pay. The treasury fell to four hundred and seventy, but the monthly loss shrank from twenty-four dīnārs to five. The debt to the estates remains, and the nobles remain sullen. But Gharnāṭa is no longer only a jewel in a narrow ring. *Say: O God, Owner of Sovereignty, You give sovereignty to whom You will.*
