Four saves cover the two wars: the declaration on Galicia (18 May), the declaration on Navarre (17 August), the day of the peaces (3 November) and the settled north two days later (5 November). Ended wars drop out of the save's war list, so the war scores (82 for Galicia, 59 for Navarre and Castile) come from the peace records. Both peaces fall on 3 November, and the save doesn't say which was signed first. In the 3 November save all 45 conquered towns belong to the crown. By 5 November they've been handed out, and the Galician ṭāʾifa's records date the handover to the 3rd as well. The kings' fates are where the 3 November save puts them: Diego Villamarín is a burgher of Augsburg, with new traits (careful, calm, naval engineer), Alfonso Gastón is at the court of Banbalūna, and Fernando of Castile and his son Juan, a knight of the Garter, are at the court of Burghush. The destroyed cathedral is still listed in the save as a work of art, destroyed on 15 July 1430, and the Pórtico de la Gloria is listed at Ishbīliya. The holy site of Santiago is still there.

Invented colour. Shant Yāqub entry: the princes arguing in private, the Emperor hearing the news among his ships, Abū al-Ḥasan's flanking ride and breaking the archers before noon, the garrison marching out under its banners, the masons told to save the porch, and the ʿulamāʾ divided between praise and silence. Mountains entry: the march down the Nervión, the Basques breaking under the horse, and the court not envying Diego Sotomaior. Closing entry: the Pórtico waiting in a storeroom, and the news the merchant ships bring. Al-Manṣūr's bells and the monk at the tomb are from the Arabic and Latin histories, and so is the return of the bells to Santiago after Córdoba fell in 1236. The location Cangas in the game's East Asturias is Cangas de Onís, below Covadonga.
