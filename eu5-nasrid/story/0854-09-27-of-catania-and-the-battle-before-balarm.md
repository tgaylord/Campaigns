The army that came ashore at Saraqūsa did not go straight for Balarm. Between it and Balarm stood Catania, under its mountain, the strongest fortress of the east coast. In Shawwāl of the year before, the Tripolitanians had thrown themselves at it and lost three men in four. On 19 Muḥarram 854 the army of al-Andalus sat down before its walls and settled in to wait.

It waited through the spring and the summer. In Jumādā al-Ākhira the Genoese, who had lost Corsica, their fleets and their appetite for the war, made their own peace with Tūnis and with the Emperor. The price of their leaving was Corsica. The island the army had spent a year taking went back to Genoa's vassal there. The soldiers before Catania said that Bastia had cost them two hundred and sixty-five days for nothing. Their officers told them it had bought Genoa's absence from Sicily, and in time the soldiers came to believe it.

Catania fell on 20 Shaʿbān 854, after two hundred and eight days. The road west to Balarm was open.

With Catania, the war changed hands. It had been begun by Tūnis, and Tūnis had been paid for its part: the Arboreans were broken, and the north of Sardinia was the Ḥafṣid sultan's for the taking. But Sicily had been won with Andalusī blood, Andalusī steel and Andalusī sweat. The Tunisians had broken themselves four times against Balarm, and al-Andalus had taken Catania within the year. The [Emperor](person:muhammad-ibn-ismail-ibn-saad) wrote to the sultan in courteous terms. [Ismāʿīl](person:ismail-ibn-muhammad-ibn-ismail) carried the letter's meaning in person. Al-Andalus would have compensation for what it had spent, and the compensation was Sicily, all of it. The sultan had begun the war for Balarm alone. He read the letter, counted the Andalusī soldiers in the field, and found that he understood the Emperor's desire. From that day the war was al-Andalus's to lead and to end.

The king of Ṣiqilliya gathered everything he had left before Balarm: nearly eight thousand men, most of them archers of the levy. On 27 Ramaḍān 854 the army of al-Andalus, eight thousand seven hundred professional soldiers, met him there. The Sicilian archers broke under the horse. When the field was cleared, the king had lost more than five thousand men killed or taken. Al-Andalus had lost eight hundred, and took in some five hundred of the enemy's men who chose the Emperor's pay over the prison. The remnant of the Sicilian host fled west along the coast with the king.

It was the twenty-seventh of Ramaḍān, and the night before had been, by many reckonings, the Night of Power. The preachers of [Ishbīliya](place:ishbiliyya) made the most of it at the Friday prayer. [Aḥmad](person:ahmad-ibn-muhammad-ibn-ismail), who had paid for the army, was asked by a courtier what he thought of the omen. He said he thought more of the thughūr.

*And God made it not but good tidings and so that your hearts would be assured thereby. And victory is not but from God. Indeed, God is Exalted in Might and Wise.*
