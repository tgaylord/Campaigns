King Diego had lost his capital, but he had not yet lost his kingdom. He gathered what men he still had in the east of it, three thousand two hundred, and in Dhū al-Qaʿda 833 he threw them at Oviedo, where the host of the Ṭāʾifa of Liyūn lay in the old capital of the Asturian kings. Liyūn's general was [Juan Cangas](person:juan-cangas), a Leonese of the faith, and he had five thousand six hundred men behind good walls. On 15 Dhū al-Qaʿda the Galicians came at him, and by evening there were none of them left. Not one man of Liyūn was lost. Jillīqiya had no army now, and its annexation was a question only of the day on which the Emperor would accept it.

The princes did not want to accept it yet. Their father had never given up his hatred of Qashtāla, and he would not rest while a Castilian king sat in Santander. But Qashtāla was under the protection of London, and the empire was not ready for a war with the English, who held France and Languedoc and the passes of the Pyrenees beside Catalonia. Then the princes saw an opening. King [Alfonso](person:alfonso-gaston) of Nabarra had allied himself with Qashtāla. Qashtāla had the protection of London, but Nabarra had none. If the empire made war on Nabarra, Qashtāla would be bound to come in on Nabarra's side, and London would have to decide whether it was bound to follow. The armies of al-Andalus were already in the north, and the war with Jillīqiya was not yet closed. If they moved before King [Edward](person:edward-v-of-england)'s council had made up its mind, they could overwhelm both kingdoms and present London with a peace already made.

On 27 Dhū al-Qaʿda 833 (17 August 1430), with the Galician war still open, the Emperor declared war on Nabarra. It was the sixth war of the Istirdād. [Fernando](person:fernando-son-of-pedro) of Qashtāla answered his ally's call as the princes had expected, and the English did not answer his. Neither Basque nor Castilian had a man under arms on the day of the declaration. The war is called the War of the Mountains, after the country it was fought in, the wet green hills of Cantabria and the Basque coast where the Christians said their kingdoms had first been born.

It took eleven weeks. The armies of the empire swept through Qashtāla's four towns without a battle worth the name, and came down the valley of the Nervión to the gates of Bilbao. There, on 16 Ṣafar 834 (3 November 1430), Nabarra made its stand with three thousand five hundred Basques, most of them archers from the villages, against four thousand six hundred and seventy of ours. The Basques were brave, as the princes had said they would be, and it did them no good. They broke under the charge of the horse, and two thousand seven hundred and fifty of them were killed or taken, for a hundred and ninety-one of ours.

That same day all three kingdoms fell. Jillīqiya, beaten past any hope, was annexed whole: its thirty-one towns, from Shant Yāqub and A Coruña to Oviedo and Llanes, passed to the crown. Nabarra and Qashtāla were annexed with it: the ten Basque towns from Bilbao and San Sebastián to Vitoria, and the last four of Qashtāla on the Cantabrian shore, Santander, Laredo, Potes and San Vicente. The court called it the Peace of Ṣafar. For a day the crown ruled two hundred and forty-one towns and three million three hundred and seventy thousand souls, from the Algarve to San Sebastián.

The kings went their separate ways. King Diego, the soldier, left the peninsula altogether and turned up at Augsburg in the German lands, among its merchants and shipwrights, a careful and quiet man of thirty-nine who now built ships instead of armies. King Alfonso of Nabarra stayed at the court of the Ṭāʾifa of [Banbalūna](place:banbaluna), where he was said to drink more than was good for him. King Fernando, the last king of Qashtāla, son of the Pedro who had lost the War of the League and the War of Jillīqiya, went with his son [Juan](person:juan-son-of-fernando) to the court of the Ṭāʾifa of Burghush. Juan was eighteen and wore the garter of the English king's order of knighthood. In Burghush, the old capital of his ancestors, he was a guest of a Muslim lord.

The new lands were not kept by the crown. The rule of the house since the Peace of Rajab had been that the far north, where the Christians were many and the Muslims few, would be held through ṭāʾifas, and the princes saw it done the same day. The eight towns of old Ashturiyās, Oviedo and Cangas among them, went to the Emperor's brother [Aḥmad](person:ahmad-ibn-ismail-ibn-saad) of Liyūn, whose son had won the battle before Shant Yāqub and whose general had won the battle at Oviedo. Santander and the Cantabrian coast went to [Abū Ḥammū](person:abu-hammu-rahhu) of Burghush, and the Basque coast to [Aḥmad ibn Yūsuf](person:ahmad-ibn-yusuf-ibn-ismail) of Banbalūna, so that the old Navarre and the new were joined under one lord of the house of Naṣr. The other twenty-three towns of Jillīqiya, from Shant Yāqub, A Coruña and Lugo to Ourense and Tui, were made a ṭāʾifa of their own and given to [Diego Sotomaior](person:diego-sotomaior), a Galician noble of the faith, thirty-one years old. He ruled half a million Galicians from the city whose shrine the Emperor had pulled down, and the court did not envy him.

That left the crown a hundred and ninety-six towns and two and a half million subjects of its own, and eight ṭāʾifas with two hundred and thirty-six towns and nearly four million more. Every town of the peninsula from Finisterre to the Pyrenees, and from the Cantabrian Sea to the Strait, now belonged to the Emperor or to a lord who owed his throne to him. The courts of the world ranked al-Andalus third among the powers, after al-Ṣīn and the Angevins.

*It is He who expelled the ones who disbelieved among the People of the Scripture from their homes at the first gathering. You did not think that they would leave, and they thought that their fortresses would protect them from Allāh; but Allāh came upon them from where they had not expected.*
