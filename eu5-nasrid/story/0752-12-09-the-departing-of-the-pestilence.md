On the ninth of Dhū al-Ḥijja in the year 752, the Day of ʿArafa, while our lord stood on the plain beyond Makka, the pestilence left the last town of the realm. No messenger brought word of it, for there was no one moment of its going. A physician in a mountain village found one morning that there was no new swelling to lance. A grave-digger in [Mālaqa](place:malaqa) found that no one came to him that day. The bells of the Christians fell silent, and the muezzins called the prayer to mosques that were fuller than they had been in years. Only later, when the scribes of the dīwān went through their reports, did they find that the last house had been cleared on that day.

The reckoning was made, and it is written in this book. Two hundred and twenty-one thousand of the realm's people were dead. Fifty-two million were dead in the whole known world from Yāyiq to the Ocean. Of every five souls in the old realm of Gharnāṭa, two were in the ground. The cities had suffered most, [Gharnāṭa](place:gharnata), Mālaqa and [al-Mariyya](place:al-mariyya), with their alleys and their markets. The houses of healing had slowed the sickness but could not stop it. It took the burghers and the scholars hardest of all, the very men who had made those cities the light of the West.

In the years that followed, the dead were not replaced. Four years after the pestilence left, the old lands of the realm counted fewer souls than they had at the reckoning. The empty houses of the Albayzín stayed empty. The realm that came out of the plague years was not the realm that went in. It was smaller in its cities and larger in its lands, more rural and more martial than before, and nearly half Christian. It had been won in the field by the sword while it was being emptied at home by the sickness.

The physicians say the pestilence has not left the world. It lingers in the East and may come again. But in Gharnāṭa no one has died of it in four years, and the court has decided that this book may be closed. What comes next must be a book of filling what was emptied, of the madrasas and the libraries, of the call to prayer carried back into the towns that had forgotten it.

Here ends the Book of the Pestilence.

*And We will surely test you with something of fear and hunger and a loss of wealth and lives and fruits, but give good tidings to the patient.*
