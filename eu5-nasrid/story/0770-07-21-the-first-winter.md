The sultan of Fās came across the water himself to take what he had demanded. Abū ʿInān Fāris was a zealot and a soldier, and he had planned many attacks in his life. The plague had marked him years before. He landed at [Qādis](place:qadis) with eight hundred men, the vanguard of the host that was to follow. On the twenty-ninth of Rabīʿ al-Awwal 770, eighteen days after the declaration, [Murād](person:murad) fell on him with three thousand three hundred. Seven hundred and forty Marīnids died and sixty were taken. Sixty-five of ours fell. [Abū ʿInān](person:abu-inan-faris) escaped to his ships. A sultan who had come to demand submission sailed home with the smallest army he had ever commanded.

Then the court did what it had promised in 761: it threw its whole weight into the war. [Marballa](place:marballa) fell on the nineteenth of Rabīʿ al-Thānī to a single assault, its garrison of thirteen overwhelmed. On the fourth of Jumādā al-Ūlā the army of the so-called Kingdom of Sevilla, eight hundred men, stood at [Jabal Ṭāriq](place:jabal-tariq) and was broken. Ten days later its last three hundred and twenty were destroyed to a man at [Ṭarīf](place:tarifa). The puppet kingdom had no army left, and its towns began to open their gates.

In the middle of that campaign news came from [Gharnāṭa](place:gharnata). [Aiman](person:aiman-mamluk) had given the sultan a second daughter, [Jadīla](person:jadila-bint-saad), named for her aunt. Seven weeks later the first fruit of the Egyptian alliance arrived in the western sea. Ten Mamlūk ships out of Barqa met a ship of Tilimsān off the eastern coast and sank it. They were the first ships of Egypt in those waters in the memory of anyone living.

The Marīnids answered with their first landing. On the twenty-ninth of Jumādā al-Ākhira two thousand six hundred came ashore at Marballa to take it back and fell on Murād's army of six thousand four hundred. Fifteen hundred and forty of them died, against five hundred and seventy of ours, and the rest were driven back into the sea. A week later the stragglers, a thousand and fifty men cut off in the mountains, attacked five hundred and seventy-five Granadans at [Runda](place:runda) and were destroyed to the last man.

By Rajab the whole northern shore was in the sultan's hands, save one place. The old fortress of Qādis, on its island at the end of its causeway, still flew the banner of Fās. The war score stood at nearly a third in Gharnāṭa's favour. But the dīwān's reckoners looked across the water and were not comforted. Fās and its allies had forty-seven thousand men under arms, twenty-three thousand of them Moroccan. Gharnāṭa had eight thousand eight hundred, and its ṭāʾifas fewer than three thousand. The Mamlūk sultan and his vassals had a hundred thousand more, but they were at the other end of the sea. The winter had been won with speed. The spring would be a test of numbers.

*And remember when you were few and oppressed in the land, fearing that people might abduct you, but He sheltered you, supported you with His victory, and provided you with good things.*
