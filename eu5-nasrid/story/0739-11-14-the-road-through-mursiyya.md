Of the war that the Marīnid sultan made upon Qashtāla, there is little to tell that gladdens the heart. In Rabīʿ al-Thānī of the year 739 the Castilians met the men of Fās beneath the walls of [Ishbīliyya](place:ishbiliyya), nearly eighteen thousand against fewer than four, and the field was theirs. That winter and spring they won again at [Shalūqa](place:shaluqa), at Ishbīliyya a second time, at [Qarmūna](place:qarmuna), where six hundred Moroccans stood against twelve thousand, and at Shalūqa once more. Five times the infidel prevailed, and five times the banners of the Maghrib went back across the plain. Tūnis entered the war in that same Rabīʿ, bringing Ṭarābulus, Qābis, Qafṣa, Tuqqurt and Bijāya behind it. On the twenty-second of Rajab our lord bound himself to the Ḥafṣids by alliance, as he was already bound to them by marriage.

Yet God gives openings where men see only walls. While the Castilian hosts were busy in the west, the armies of the alliance held Ishbīliyya itself, and the Tunisians took [Mursiyya](place:mursiyya) with its fortress and the country around it. For a season, therefore, there ran a road from our own marches eastward to the frontiers of Arghūn, where a Christian king holds lands that were once al-Andalus. Our lord knew that such a road would not stay open long.

In the court there was one small sorrow. On the fourth of Dhū al-Qaʿda [Gideon ben Joshua](person:gideon-ben-joshua), the Jewish burgher, a man quick with figures and quicker with the cup, missed his footing on a stair and did not rise again.

Ten days later, on the fourteenth of Dhū al-Qaʿda in the year 739, the claim was made good. Gharnāṭa would reclaim from Arghūn what belonged to al-Andalus, and [war](war:war-on-aragon) was proclaimed, the first that Gharnāṭa had made in its own name in this reign. Command of the host went to [Muḥammad ibn al-Khaṭīb](person:ibn-al-khatib), who was twenty-five years old and had no equal at court in counsel or in arms. Under him marched nine thousand five hundred levies of the towns and the mountains, nine hundred regulars of the sultan, and four hundred hired swords. With them went a baggage train of three hundred and fifty, so that the army might still eat when the road through Mursiyya closed behind it.

If the hand that writes these lines seems to know that commander too well, let the reader pardon it. *And whoever puts his trust in God, He suffices him.*
