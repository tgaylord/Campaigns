In Shaʿbān 782 the Emperor's mother died. [Sayyida](person:sayyida-of-fezzan), daughter of the lords of Fezzan, had been betrothed to [Yūsuf](person:yusuf-i) at nine years old. She came to [Gharnāṭa](place:gharnata) across the desert and the sea and outlived her husband by seventeen years. She sat in her son's dīwān through the worst of his wars and lived to see him proclaimed Emperor. She was fifty-two. The court mourned her as a mother of the dynasty. Her son mourned her as his mother, and for some weeks he would see no one.

A year later news came from [Fās](place:fas) that [Abū ʿInān Fāris](person:abu-inan-faris) was dead. He had ruled the Marīnids for thirty-six years and twice made war on the house of Naṣr to bring it to heel, and twice failed. His son [Abū al-Faḍl](person:abu-al-fadl-marinid) took the throne, and with him the Emperor's sister [Jadīla](person:jadila-bint-yusuf) became queen of the Marīnids. The alliance [Duʿāʾ](person:dua-marinid) had argued for was now a family matter on both sides of the Strait.

The peace held for four years and then broke from inside. On 24 Ramaḍān 784, as the city kept the last nights of the fast, [Ṭulayṭula](place:tulaytula) rose. The Castilians of the old Visigothic capital drove the Emperor's governor out of the city and wrecked the madrasa the crown had built in its heart. They proclaimed a Kingdom of Ṭulayṭula, and the country followed them. Fifty-eight towns went over: the Middle March and La Mancha, the hills of Extremadura, and even the lands of [Mursiyya](place:mursiyya) and [Jayyān](place:jayyan), Ubbadha and Bayyāsa, and the coast of Walba. Half a million souls were in revolt, most of them Castilians and three in four of them Christians. The rebels raised nearly six thousand men, most of them peasant levies, against the Emperor's thirty-seven hundred regulars. The court called it another so-called kingdom, as it had called the rising of [Ishbīliya](place:ishbiliyya) thirty years before. It was no small matter.

The Emperor's younger brother [Ismāʿīl](person:ismail-ibn-yusuf), twenty-eight, took their mother's empty seat in the dīwān as the revolt began. The Emperor took the field. The rebels came to him in Dhū al-Ḥijja, at Munt Shāqir in the hills above the Guadiana, four thousand nine hundred against his ten thousand six hundred, and he broke them there. It was the only real battle of the revolt. The rest was a hunt. On 26 Ṣafar 785 the Emperor caught the last rebel host, one thousand eight hundred and seven men, outside the walls of Jayyān, and destroyed it to the last man. Forty-four of his own fell. The rebels' kingdom had lasted five months.

The towns came back to their obedience one by one. The governor went back to Ṭulayṭula, and the masons went back to the madrasa. The Emperor, with the realm secure for the first time in ten years, began to plan the pilgrimage he had put off since 774.

*So do not weaken and do not grieve, and you will be superior if you are believers.*
