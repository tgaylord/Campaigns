While Gharnāṭa buried its dead, the war it had left went on without it. By Rabīʿ al-Thānī of the year 751 Castile had found five thousand men among the living. With them it drove the Marīnid raiders back from its heartlands, where they had burned and taken captives at will for two summers. Yet in the lands of Aragon and on its islands the ships of Tūnis and the other Moorish lords went where they pleased, and sacked the countryside from the Ebro to Mayūrqa. Aragon had won every battle at sea in the war's first years, and since the day off Cape Ben Sakka it had no fleet to stop them.

On the seventh of Shawwāl Aragon made its peace with the Muslim powers. Morocco took the Balearic Islands, Mayūrqa, Manūrqa and Yābisa, which had been lost to the Christians more than a century before. Tūnis took Aragon's vassal in Sardinia, the County of Cagliari, and made it its own subject. On the first day of the new Christian year the mainland lands of the king of Majorca, Perpinyà, Prades and Puigcerdà in the mountains, and Montpellier, passed to Aragon. So the kingdom of Majorca was no more, divided between the Cross and the Crescent.

The Marīnids had their islands, and they had them because Gharnāṭa had broken Castile's armies in the field. They did not say so. Now there was only Castile left in the war, and all eyes turned to the Strait. The Castilians pushed south to [Qādis](place:qadis) and the ports of the Strait, and drove the Moroccans from this shore. The Marīnid sultan, who had begun the war to reclaim al-Andalus, held islands in the sea and no foot of al-Andalus itself.
