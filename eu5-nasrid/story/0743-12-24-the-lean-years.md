The years 742 and 743 have no battles in them, and so the chroniclers of other courts would pass over them in a line. This chronicle will not, for in those years the state was mended. Mending is slower work than war and harder to praise.

The debt to the estates was the first matter. It had been taken on in the war with Arghūn and it hung on the crown like a chain, for every dīnār owed to the nobles was a dīnār's worth of their say in the realm's affairs. Year by year the sultan paid it down, and year by year the treasury stood empty to do it. By Dhū al-Ḥijja of 743 twelve more loans had been repaid, and the debt stood at four hundred and thirty-seven, less than half what it had been when the war with Arghūn ended. The treasury held four dīnārs.

In Dhū al-Qaʿda of 742 the sultan wrote across the sea to the Ḥafṣid sultan of Tūnis. He spoke of the struggle for the Strait and of the burden of a lone Muslim kingdom on the Christian shore. The Ḥafṣid answered as a brother and sent two hundred and fifty-eight gold pieces. It was not a great sum, but he asked nothing in return, and the court did not forget it.

In Rabīʿ al-Awwal of 743 the Catalan merchant privileges came back into force. They had been granted again in the days after the peace with Arghūn, and the merchants of Barshalūna returned to the silk markets of Gharnāṭa and Mālaqa with their ships and their credit. The nobles and the burghers both lost some of their weight in the realm by it, and the crown gained. In Rabīʿ al-Thānī the dīwān's quiet grievance against Qashtāla was complete: the realm now had a just cause for a war of independence, written and sealed, whenever it should choose to use it. It was locked away.

In Shaʿbān the sultan issued the Royal Decree, by which the crown might command where before it had only asked. Its power grew by a tenth, and the realm began, slowly, to bend toward the throne. Meanwhile [Ismāʿīl ibn Ismāʿīl](person:ismail-ibn-ismail) had turned his seat in the dīwān back to the defence of the realm's sovereignty. It stood a hair above the half, and the scales of influence had moved halfway back toward the middle of the channel.

In the house of Raḥḥū a son was born. [Khālid](person:khalid-ibn-yahya) was the child of [Yaḥyā Raḥḥū](person:yahya-rahhu), the commander of the Volunteers, and the lady [Qamar](person:qamar), widow of the sultan Ismāʿīl and mother of Ismāʿīl ibn Ismāʿīl. So the house of the Volunteers' general was joined to the house of Naṣr through the old sultan's widow. Some at court approved of it and some did not.

In the West the Portuguese war went on, and some in the dīwān hoped it might be turned to the realm's use. But Queen [Maria](person:maria-of-portugal) was the wife of [Alfonso](person:alfonso-xi) of Qashtāla, and their son [Peter](person:peter-of-castile) was heir to both crowns. Any blow at Portugal was a blow at the house of Qashtāla. And in the Ṭāʾifas the call to prayer spread on. Balansiyya now counted sixty-six thousand Muslims, and Mursiyya was nearly half Muslim.

*And whoever fears God, He will make for him a way out, and will provide for him from where he does not expect.*
