On 14 Rabīʿ al-Thānī 851 a daughter was born in the house of the lords of Banbalūna. Her father was Saʿd, son of [Aḥmad](person:ahmad-ibn-yusuf-ibn-ismail) of Banbalūna. Her mother was Muʾmina, daughter of the prince [Aḥmad](person:ahmad-ibn-muhammad-ibn-ismail). They named her Fāṭima. The court counted her twice over: a great-granddaughter of the Emperor, and one more knot between the crown and the ṭāʾifas of the north.

Her great-grandmother lived to hear of her and not much longer. The lady [Isabel de Montsoriu](person:isabel-de-montsoriu) had come south from the Catalan houses as a girl. She was the Emperor's wife for more than forty years and the mother of his son Aḥmad, and through her own mother she was a granddaughter of the house of Naṣr. She died of old age on 6 Jumādā al-Ākhira 851, on the day she turned sixty-five.

The Emperor [Muḥammad](person:muhammad-ibn-ismail-ibn-saad) mourned her with the ceremony he gave to everything, and it was real for all that. Aḥmad mourned her by working. His mother had been the first to praise the cities he drew in the sand as a boy. In the months after her death he spent the treasury almost to the bottom.

The treasury had been full in the spring. By Rajab it held less than a tenth of what it had held in Muḥarram, and the clerks could show where every dirham had gone. Six bridges went up in the country of the Tagus and the Guadiana, at al-Qanṭara, Madallīn, the Serena, Valencia de Alcántara, Brozas and Jaraicejo, so that the roads of the west no longer waited on the seasons for a ford. The townsmen of al-Qanṭara already had a bridge, the old Roman one, which had given the town its name. Aḥmad gave them a second. The gunsmiths of [Jayyān](place:jayyan), [Baṭalyaws](place:batalyaws) and [Shantarīn](place:shantarin) took on more hands. New tanneries opened at [Qurṭuba](place:qurtuba) and [Lārida](place:larida), and new glassworks at [Ishbīliya](place:ishbiliyya). There was a distillery at [Sharīsh](place:sharish), a lumber mill at [Istijja](place:istijja), and a press for the wine of the Christian vineyards at Castellón. At [Jarunda](place:jarunda) a second foundry began to cast cannon. The Emperor asked what it all cost and was told. He let his son go on.

The court had grown in other ways. In those months it took in a scholar of Delhi named Dilipapala, a scholar of ʿIrāq, and a lord of the Tandroy from the great island beyond the Cape, Matoky of the Fahambahy. He was the first man of Madagascar most of the court had ever seen, and the pages followed him through the palace. No one wrote down how he had come.

On 20 Rajab [ʿAbd al-Jalīl al-Qubbāṣī](person:abd-al-jalil-al-qubbasi) came to the end of his commission. Four days later he sailed again for the cape where Africa ends, this time to find a place where the crown could leave men behind.

*And We have enjoined upon man care for his parents. His mother carried him in weakness upon weakness, and his weaning is in two years. Be grateful to Me and to your parents; to Me is the destination.*
