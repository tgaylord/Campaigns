The crusade ended, and the arsenals went on rising. Two years after the Emperor [Muḥammad](person:muhammad-ibn-ismail-ibn-saad) had signed the orders, all seven were finished, at [Ishbīliya](place:ishbiliyya), [Mālaqa](place:malaqa), [al-Mariyya](place:al-mariyya), [al-Ushbūna](place:al-ushbuna), [Balansiyya](place:balansiyya), Cartagena and [Mursiyya](place:mursiyya). Their first work was already at sea: eight war galleys of a new Andalusī pattern, long, low and fast under oar, the first warships of the empire not copied from the Christians. They sailed beside the fifty caravels the fleet already had. A new guild of naval chandlers was licensed at Cáceres, far inland, to send pitch and cordage down the river roads to the ports, and the treasurers, who had complained of every arsenal, found that the realm took in ninety dīnārs a month more than it spent.

The Emperor had given up the admiral's command before his pilgrimage, but he had not given up the sea, and he did not let the realm give it up either. He gave the arsenals' masters seats at his audiences. He gave his prizes for poetry to poems about ships. He sent the sons of the umarāʾ to serve a season with the fleet before he would give them a command on land. In the three years after the crusade the court's talk, and the talk of the cities, turned further from the land to the sea than in all the reign of his father. The young men of Lisbon and the Algarve wanted to be pilots. The merchants of Ishbīliya wanted ships of their own. In Mālaqa the ʿulamāʾ were heard to preach that al-Andalus lay between two seas as the believer lies between fear and hope, and that it would live by both.

The sea took its price. [Berenguer Ramon](person:berenguer-ramon-de-montsoriu) of Catalonia, who had beaten the Mecklenburgers off Cape Mondego, was lost at sea two months later, in Muḥarram 836, and his son [Francesc](person:francesc-de-montsoriu) took his seat in Barshalūna. Less than three years later the sea took the crusaders' admiral [Vukmir Janković](person:vukmir-jankovic) too, and the captains of Ishbīliya drank to him in their own fashion, with sherbet.

By Shawwāl 839 the Emperor's ships were reaching further than they had ever gone. A new town of the crown had been planted at Ipalán on La Gomera in the Canaries, and an expedition was fitting out at Ishbīliya for the open Ocean. Two men of al-Ushbūna led the work. [ʿAbd al-Jalīl al-Qbasī](person:abd-al-jalil-al-qbasai) was an Andalusī of nineteen, a born soldier with the explorer's eye. Dom [Raimundo Coimbra](person:raimundo-coimbra) was a Portuguese Muslim of twenty-six, and in that month he was somewhere out on the high sea of the eastern Ocean, beyond sight of any land, toward the waters where the pilots of Flores had said that the sun set over nothing at all.

*He released the two seas, meeting side by side; between them is a barrier which neither of them transgresses. So which of the favours of your Lord would you deny? From both of them emerge pearl and coral.*
