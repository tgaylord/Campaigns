The sultan had doubted that a remitted tax would buy a loyal heart, and he was right. On the twenty-fifth of Dhū al-Ḥijja in the year 753, as the year ran out, the Castilian peasants of the new lands rose together. In [Ishbīliyya](place:ishbiliyya) they proclaimed a kingdom of their own, which they called the Kingdom of Sevilla. In a single day every town won at the Peace of Qurṭuba, and the two bought in 751, went over to it. [Qurṭuba](place:qurtuba) of the caliphs, Ishbīliyya and [Jayyān](place:jayyan) were lost, and [Qarmūna](place:qarmuna) and the silver of [Qusanṭīna](place:qusantina) with them: twenty-five towns and three hundred and twenty-eight thousand souls. Gharnāṭa woke to find itself what it had been before the war with the three crowns, eighteen towns in the mountains and on the sea.

Then came the thing that made the court laugh in the middle of its fear. The rebels were Castilians who had risen against a Muslim lord. Yet the tyrant of Qashtāla did not march to help them. He marched against them, for Gharnāṭa was still his tributary in name, and a lord who will not defend his tributary will soon have none. The ṭāʾifas of Balansiyya and Mursiyya took the field beside us as well. But the laughter did not last long, for the dīwān saw the danger at once. If the Castilian host reached Ishbīliyya first, it would not take the city back for Gharnāṭa. It would take the city back for Qashtāla, and the Peace of Qurṭuba would be undone by the very king who had sealed it.

So our lord [Yūsuf](person:yusuf-i) did not wait for his overlord. The rebels had taken the land, but they could not raise men to hold it, for the plague had emptied their villages as it had emptied ours. On the third of Jumādā al-Ūlā in the year 754 they gathered what they had, some four and a half thousand, and fell on the army of Gharnāṭa at Sharīsh. They broke against it and left two thousand three hundred on the field. On the twenty-fourth of that month the sultan's horse caught their last army, two thousand men, at Shalūqa al-Kubrā on the road below Ishbīliyya. Not one of them came away, and the sultan lost thirteen men.

The so-called kingdom had no army left, and its towns opened their gates. On the second of Jumādā al-Ākhira the revolt was ended. Qashtāla came to the peace with nothing in its hands, since Gharnāṭa had done the fighting, and it could only reaffirm the border it had sealed four years before. All twenty-five towns returned to the house of Naṣr. The realm was forty-three towns again, six hundred thousand souls, and fifty-five of the seventy-two towns of the Strait's three lands. Its sovereignty stood higher than it had ever stood in the reign.

The court praised the speed of the sultan's horse. The wiser heads remembered how the revolt had begun. The Castilians of the valley of the great river had not been won by the remitted tax and would not be won by the victory. They had been beaten, and that is not the same thing. The sultan's thoughts of moving his throne to Qurṭuba were not abandoned. But no one now imagined it would be easy to sit there.

*Perhaps you dislike a thing and it is good for you, and perhaps you love a thing and it is bad for you. And God knows, while you know not.*
