By the end of Ramaḍān 776, [Barshalūna](place:barshaluna) had been under siege for two hundred and eighty days. The walls on the landward side were breached in three places, and the townsmen were eating what townsmen eat when the bread is gone. King [Joan](person:joan-of-aragon) knew how few days he had left. He was not finished, though. He still wore the crown of Ṣiqilliya, and the Sicilians had an army of five thousand levies and the fleet that had sunk Barqa's ships off Māliṭa. A king with an island behind him could lose his capital and still go on fighting, and the war might go on for years.

The shaykh of Barqa ended that hope. [Ramaḍān](person:ramadan-of-barqa) had lost his ships, so he found others. In the last weeks of the fast he put three thousand men ashore on Ṣiqilliya itself and took Saraqūsa by storm, the old city of the Greeks on the island's eastern shore. The Sicilian levies could probably have driven his three thousand back into the sea. But the sultan's envoys, when they came to King Joan in the days after the feast of the breaking of the fast, asked him what he would do about the next landing, and the one after that.

They gave him a choice. He could make peace. Arghūn would pass to the sultan. Māliṭa would pass to the Mamlūks. And the king himself would go to Balarm unharmed and free, with his household and his Sicilian crown. Or the war could go on in Ṣiqilliya. The island had been a land of Islam for two hundred years before the Normans took it. The envoys said its peasants would soon show whether they remembered the faith of their forefathers as well as [Qurṭuba](place:qurtuba) had. Joan was greedy, but he was not a fool. On 11 Shawwāl 776 he accepted, and sailed for Balarm.

By the time the peace was sealed, Arghūn had already come apart. Its outlying lordships had gone their own ways in the last months of the war. Montpellier, beyond the mountains, swore itself to the king of Inkiltara rather than the king of Ifranja. Sassari, on the island of Sardāniya, was taken back by the judges of Arborea, who were vassals of Janawa. What was left was thirty-four towns from the Pyrenees to the Ebro: Barshalūna and [Ṭarrakūna](place:tarrakuna), [Jarunda](place:jarunda) under the mountains, [Lārida](place:larida) on the Segre, [Ṭurṭūsha](place:turtusha) at the Ebro's mouth, Perpignan beyond the passes, and the hill towns of the old Aragonese marches at Fraga, Munt Shūn and Barbashturu. Half a million souls lived in them, nearly all of them Catalan and Christian. All of it passed to the sultan, and the Kingdom of Arghūn held no land at all. The courtiers called it the end of Arghūn. The sultan said: for now.

The dīwān's reckoners brought him the new count, and it made the old courtiers uneasy. The realm now had ninety-four towns and one million two hundred and seventy-five thousand souls. For the first time, the Catalans outnumbered the Andalusīs in the sultan's own lands, four hundred and fifty-five thousand to four hundred and fifty-two thousand. The Christians outnumbered the faithful two to one. Barshalūna, with forty-six thousand people, was the third city of the realm after [Ishbīliya](place:ishbiliyya) and [Gharnāṭa](place:gharnata). The war had cost the sultan one thousand two hundred and sixty men in battle and Arghūn four thousand seven hundred, besides those the siege and the winter took. In fifteen months the sultan had more than doubled the number of Christians who called him lord.

Our lord [Saʿd](person:saad-ibn-yusuf) did not mean to keep it that way. He had read the Survey of the Kingdoms. He knew what Ifranja, Qashtāla and the Pope at Avignon would say of a Muslim sultan who swallowed a Christian kingdom whole. So he told the dīwān that the crown would not keep Catalonia and Arghūn. They would become two new ṭāʾifas, like [Balansiyya](place:balansiyya) and al-Gharb before them. One would be Catalonia on the coast. The other would be Arghūn itself, raised again in the interior, a successor kingdom to the one that had died. The sultan would not be seen to have seized Aragonese land. He would be seen to have returned it to its rightful heirs, under his protection. In the same reordering, [Mursiyya](place:mursiyya), a ṭāʾifa for thirty-five years, would be taken into the crown directly.

The old men of the dīwān who had studied the caliphs' map in the Hall of Ambassadors understood what came next. The War of the Upper March had been won without [Saraqusṭa](place:saraqusta), the Upper March's own capital. Saraqusṭa and the heart of old Arghūn, from the Ebro to the mountains of Jaca, were held by Qashtāla. A restored Arghūn would have a claim to them, and its protector would have a cause. The truce with Qashtāla would end on the fourth day of Muḥarram 777. The sultan's scribes were already drafting the claim. He meant the world to read it not as a war of Gharnāṭa, but as the restoration of Arghūn.

*But if they intend to deceive you, then sufficient for you is Allāh. It is He who supported you with His help and with the believers.*
