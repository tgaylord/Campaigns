A state cannot be remade without being shaken. In the year after the Jamāʿa the realm grew unquiet. Its stability, which had stood high in the lean years, fell by Ramaḍān of 745 to a fifth of what it had been. The cause was the mending itself. The Jamāʿa's reforms were tearing down the old order of the realm and raising a new one in its place: a new Distribution of Power, a new custom at court, old offices and old rights unsettled. And all the while the debt to the estates gnawed at the crown's authority, for a lord who owes his nobles money cannot command them as a lord who does not. The realm was being rebuilt with the roof still on, and everyone under it felt the walls move.

Through it all the sultan held to his course. Six more loans were repaid in that year, and the debt fell to two hundred and eighty-eight dīnārs. The crown now took in more each month than it spent, by nine dīnārs. The realm's sovereignty stood at the barest margin above the half, and the scales of influence hung almost at the middle of the channel. Gharnāṭa leaned neither toward Qashtāla nor toward Fās.

In the Ṭāʾifas the work of the sultan's envoys bore fruit. [Joan d'Oriola](person:joan-doriola) of [Balansiyya](place:balansiyya) was counted a loyal servant of the throne, and the land he ruled counted eighty thousand Muslims to a hundred and one thousand Christians. [Juan Manuel Enríquez](person:juan-manuel-enriquez), whose Ṭāʾifa of Mursiyya was still only [Jumalla](place:jumalla), was still sullen, though two in three of his people now prayed toward Mecca. At court a man of the *ʿulamāʾ* named [Najīb](person:najib) came to notice. He was a man of rare gifts in counsel, in diplomacy and in arms, and stubborn in all three, and the court marked him as one to watch.

The house of Naṣr cast its net wide. In Rabīʿ al-Awwal of 746 a betrothal was agreed with the Ḥafṣids of Tūnis, who had sent gold when gold was needed. In Jumādā al-Ūlā another was agreed with the kings of Ghāna, beyond the great desert, whose gold comes up the caravan roads to the markets of the Maghrib. The sultan meant to have friends on every shore.

By Rabīʿ al-Thānī of 747 the debt was all but gone. Four small loans remained, a hundred and twenty dīnārs or less, where once there had been nearly a thousand. The reforms had gathered power to the crown, and the realm began at last to steady. Stability rose again, to ten. The state the young sultan had found in debt and tribute ten years before was mended. It was not healed, but it was whole and it was standing.

It was given eleven weeks to enjoy it. On the twenty-eighth of Jumādā al-Ākhira in the year 747 the Marīnid sultan declared war on Qashtāla once again, and the three crowns of the Christians answered as one. Here ends the Book of the Mending of the State.

*So verily, with hardship there is ease. Verily, with hardship there is ease.*
