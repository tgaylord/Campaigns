This note covers two entries: Catania and the battle before Palermo, and the peace and the duke of Athens.

**What the saves show.** There are two saves for this stretch: an autosave of 1 February 1450 and a save of 29 November 1450, the day the peace was signed.

*Before the landing.* By February 1450 the army had grown from 9 regiments to 13, and the fleet had 95 ships, with the hulks doubled to 20 by November. The treasury was 2,990 in February, up from 175 in August 1449. As with the jump in 1447, the saves don't show where the money came from.

*The peace.* The war is gone from the November save. The terms are read from the map and the treaties:
- All 22 towns of Sicily are now al-Andalus's.
- Seven of Arborea's towns in northern Sardinia went to Tunis. Arborea keeps Oristano.
- Corsica is back with Genoa's vassal. Genoa's truce dates from 15 July 1450, so the entry puts its separate peace then.
- Athens and Negroponte became vassals of al-Andalus on 29 November 1450.
- Negroponte's ruler is now the Emperor himself, with Ismāʿīl as its heir. In the February save Sicily's king Peter, of the house of Barcelona, ruled both Sicily and Negroponte.
- Athens's duke Peter (born 1399) is also of the house of Barcelona. He commanded the Athenian ship that attacked Yūsuf off Terracina on 5 January 1448.
- Naples has held a casus belli on al-Andalus since 29 November 1450.
- The truce with Sicily and the Pope runs to 1465.
- Palermo comes with a grand palace, a cathedral, a castle and a canal lock. The entry calls the palace the Norman kings', which is real history.

*The battles.* The war's later battles are lost from the save once the war ends. Palermo and Sciacca come from Trent's battle screens; Catania (13 December 1449) and the Gulf of Oristano (14 November 1449) come from the February autosave. Neither screen names our commander. The Sicilian commander's portrait is crowned, and King Peter commanded at Palermo in 1448 and 1449, so the entries have the king lead his army.

*The Mamlūk war.* It goes on. The save adds 23 battles to it between August 1449 and October 1450, with fortunes swinging both ways. Two of them, al-Khanbarah and al-Ukhaydir, couldn't be placed on the map.

**From Trent's account.** The siege of Catania (208 days, ending 28 September 1450). The change of war aims ("Andalusi blood, Andalusi steel, Andalusi sweat"), with Tunis understanding. Athens's offer of fealty for autonomy. The plan to raise a Sicilian ṭāʾifa once the Mamlūk war ends.

**Invented colour.**

*Catania entry:*
- The soldiers' and officers' view of Corsica.
- The Emperor's letter to the sultan, and Ismāʿīl carrying it.
- The archers breaking under the horse.
- Prisoners taking the Emperor's pay. The screen shows men taken in, but not why.
- The Night of Power and the preachers.
- Aḥmad's remark.

*Peace entry:*
- The flight toward Trapani.
- The Pope's indifference.
- The Catalan duchy's history. The Catalan Company is real history.
- Yūsuf speaking for the duke.
- The Emperor's delight in Negroponte and his map.
- The dīwān filing Naples's claim.
- Aḥmad counselling the delay of the ṭāʾifa.
