In Jumādā al-Ūlā 812 the house of Naṣr lost [Fāṭima](person:fatima-bint-yusuf), the daughter of Yūsuf I who had married into the Catalan house of Montsoriu. She had been given to Francesc de Montsoriu in the year of the Restoration, when the Ṭāʾifa of Catalonia was made, and she left behind a son who ruled Catalonia, a daughter married to the lord of Shalamanka, and another, [Isabel](person:isabel-de-montsoriu), who was the wife of the heir. So the blood of Yūsuf I ran in three of the ṭāʾifas and in the crown itself. In Dhū al-Ḥijja of the same year a son was born to the Emperor's son [Saʿd](person:saad-ibn-ismail-ibn-saad) and the Shaykha [Maryam](person:maryam-bint-saad-ibn-faraj) of [Qūnka](place:qunka). They named him [Abū al-Ḥasan ʿAlī](person:abu-al-hasan-ali-ibn-saad), and the frontier had its first heir of the new line.

The Peace of Rajab had filled the treasury and emptied the kingdom of Portugal, and it left the Emperor [Ismāʿīl](person:ismail-ibn-saad) ruling more Christians than any Muslim prince since the conquest. His father had answered the north with madrasas at [al-Ushbūna](place:al-ushbuna) and [Shantarīn](place:shantarin) and a handful of other towns. Ismāʿīl meant to answer it everywhere. On 11 Shawwāl 815, ten days after the feast at the end of Ramaḍān, he ordered a madrasa built in every town and city of the empire, and he did not stop at the crown's own lands. The ṭāʾifas were to have them too, from Burtuqāl to Banbalūna, and the gold taken from Qashtāla would pay for them.

The ʿulamāʾ had never been given so much at once, and they said so from every minbar. The lords of the ṭāʾifas were less pleased to find the Emperor's teachers in their towns, but none of them could refuse a gift paid for by the Emperor's own treasury. In the dīwān the Emperor gave his reasons in the plain way he kept for matters he meant to see done. A conquered town that has a garrison obeys while the garrison is there, he said. A town that has a madrasa will obey after the garrison has gone, and its children will not need to be conquered at all.

His second order came on 29 Muḥarram 816, and he made it in honour of his father. The Emperor [Saʿd](person:saad-ibn-yusuf) had sent the costliest tutors in the Islamic world to his sons and paid the estates every dirham he owed them, and the clerks had written of him that he left the country a more honest place. Ismāʿīl now gave his father's realm a law of the Education of the People. Its preamble said that knowledge was the right of every subject, and that all men are born with free will and free thought, which the state must protect. The burgher, the peasant, the labourer and the soldier were all to have their chance at letters. The umarāʾ grumbled that a ploughman who could read would soon stop ploughing. The ʿulamāʾ, who had just been given a madrasa in every town, grumbled that the law spoke of free thought and not of faith. The Emperor told them both that his father would have signed it, and signed it.

*And say: My Lord, increase me in knowledge.*
