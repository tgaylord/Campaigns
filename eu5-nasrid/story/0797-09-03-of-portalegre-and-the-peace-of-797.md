With Qashtāla's armies gone, the war passed to Portugal. Its king was [Jerónimo](person:jeronimo-of-castile), fifty-two, who had once worn the crowns of Qashtāla and Portugal together and had kept only the second when they parted. He had lost a war to the Emperor already, in 778, and he had no wish to lose another. In the spring he gathered every man Portugal could raise, fourteen thousand eight hundred and eighty-one of them, and waited on the Alentejo border at Portalegre.

The Emperor came to him on 10 Shaʿbān 797 with fourteen thousand six hundred, the Aragonese riders of Peñafiel and the Mamlūk contingents beside him. The armies were near enough equal that the Portuguese had reason to hope, and for eighteen days they held. On 28 Shaʿbān they broke, with seven thousand and eighty-seven of theirs lost for three thousand seven hundred and forty-four of ours. The scribes of the dīwān reckoned the war complete. There was nothing left that the Christians could put in the field, and nothing left that the Emperor could not take.

On the third day of Ramaḍān, King [Pedro](person:pedro-of-castile) of Qashtāla asked for peace, and Portugal, which had gone to war for Qashtāla, was made to sign beside him.

Qashtāla gave up fifty-one towns. In the west it lost [Shalamanka](place:shalamanka) on the Tormes, the largest of them, with Ávila behind its walls, Ciudad Rodrigo, Ledesma and the Leonese country to the Portuguese border. Beyond the Guadarrama it lost [Majrīṭ](place:majrit), the fortress the amīr Muḥammad built on the Manzanares five hundred years before to guard the road to [Ṭulayṭula](place:tulaytula), and Alcalá, Guadalajara and Sigüenza, where Valencia's army had died. To the east went [Qūnka](place:qunka), Molina and Requena, and to the south-west Cáceres, Plasencia and Coria, the upper country of the old Lower March. Qashtāla would also pay reparations for ten years.

Portugal gave up twenty: Setúbal and the Sado, [Yābura](place:yabura) and the Alentejo from Elvas to Portalegre, [Shantarīn](place:shantarin) on the Tagus, and [al-Ushbūna](place:al-ushbuna) itself. Al-Ushbūna had been a city of al-Andalus for four hundred and thirty years before the crusaders took it, and now it was one again after two hundred and fifty. Its mosques had become churches, and they would stay churches. The court said so plainly at the signing, for the Emperor meant al-Ushbūna to know at once what convivencia meant.

All seventy-one towns passed directly to the crown. The realm grew in a day from a hundred and eighteen towns to a hundred and eighty-nine, and from one million two hundred and ninety-one thousand souls to two million three hundred and thirty-four thousand. Between the declaration and the peace the realm had lost nearly a hundred and fifty thousand of its own souls. In the land battles alone nineteen thousand had died on our side, counting the ṭāʾifas and the allies, and thirty-two thousand on theirs. The scribes added up the new figures more slowly than the old. There were one million one hundred thousand Castilians in the empire now, nearly three hundred thousand Leonese and three hundred thousand Portuguese, and only half a million Andalusīs. The faithful numbered five hundred and thirty-three thousand. The Christians numbered one million six hundred and seventy-eight thousand.

No Muslim ruler had held so much of the peninsula since the caliphs. And no Muslim ruler had ever ruled so many Christians. The foreign courts watched the empire to see whether it would choke on what it had swallowed, and for a season they ranked it lower than they had before the war. In the dīwān the question was already how such lands should be governed, and by whom. The ṭāʾifas of the north had taught the court that Christians would obey a Christian king who owed his crown to the Emperor more readily than a Muslim governor, and there was talk of new ṭāʾifas between the Duero and the Tagus.

*Allāh has promised those among you who believe and do righteous deeds that He will surely make them successors upon the earth, as He made those before them successors.*
