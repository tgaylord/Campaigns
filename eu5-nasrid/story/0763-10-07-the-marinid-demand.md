For twenty years the court had worked to set the Christian crowns against one another. In Rabīʿ al-Thānī of the year 760 the work bore fruit. Qashtāla and Portugal marched east together into Arghūn, and Arghūn called Savoy and Sicily to its side. The dīwān counted the dead of that war as the reports came in, ten thousand Castilians and thirty thousand Portuguese, and began to speak openly of independence. The truce with Qashtāla still had four years to run. If the Christians bled each other white before it ended, Gharnāṭa would strike.

The house of Naṣr lost another prince to a foreign court that spring. [Ismāʿīl ibn Ismāʿīl](person:ismail-ibn-ismail), the sultan's brother, married his Aydınid bride and went east to live in her father's palace in Anatolia. As with Faraj, the court said it was the harem's doing. His seat went to [Khālid](person:khalid-ibn-yahya), son of the late Yaḥyā Raḥḥū. He was sixteen years old and one of the ablest men the court had seen in a generation.

Then Fās spoiled everything. On the tenth of Shaʿbān 761, with the friendship between the two courts at its warmest, the Marīnid sultan declared war on Gharnāṭa to demand its submission as his subject. Tilimsān, Tāfīlālt and Tuwāt marched with him. The court could hardly believe it. The ambassadors had been feasted in Fās that very spring. But a sultan who sees two crowns exhausted and a third alone does not care what his ambassadors ate. Qashtāla, as Gharnāṭa's overlord, came into the war to defend it. The bitterest men at court said that the great chance had been thrown away. Instead of striking at an exhausted Qashtāla, Gharnāṭa would spend its men holding off the Marīnids until Qashtāla recovered.

The Marīnids found the shore of al-Andalus harder to hold than their fleet had promised. The first landing, two thousand men of Tilimsān at [al-Mariyya](place:al-mariyya), was thrown back into the sea by ten thousand Granadans on the twenty-sixth of Dhū al-Ḥijja. At [Sharīsh](place:sharish), in Jumādā al-Ūlā 762, they attacked an army four times their number and left fifteen hundred dead. Twice before [Ishbīliyya](place:ishbiliyya) they broke themselves on the sultan's lines. Elsewhere they did as they pleased. They landed at [Qādis](place:qadis) and on the coast of [Walba](place:walba), and their horsemen rode through Qashtāla as far as El Escorial and Cáceres, breaking every Castilian army they met. Their fleets ruled the sea.

So in the summer of 763 our lord sent a message to Fās, and it was not a plea. If Gharnāṭa threw its full weight into this war, he wrote, Fās would lose it. If Gharnāṭa stood aside, Fās would very likely beat Qashtāla. The Marīnids understood. On the seventh of Shawwāl they granted a white peace and promised to respect the lands of Gharnāṭa. Qashtāla was left to fight on alone. The ṭāʾifas of Balansiyya and Mursiyya had been kept out of the war entirely, and they would come untouched to the war that the court still meant to fight.

*And if they incline to peace, then incline to it also, and rely upon God.*
