This note covers three entries: the envoys and the Umayyad borders, the lady Isabel and the bridges, and the war for Palermo.

**What the saves show.** Two saves, 28 March and 5 October 1447.
- *The treasury:* 570 in the autosave of 1 February, 2,680 on 28 March, 216 on 5 October. Income runs at about 425–435 a month against about 365–380 out, and there is no debt in any of them, so the saves don't say where the 2,100 between February and March came from. The entry says only that the treasury was full in the spring. Trent, if you know, it's worth a line.
- *Where it went:* between March and October, six bridges (Alcántara, Medellín, Villanueva de la Serena, Valencia de Alcántara, Brozas, Jaraicejo); gunsmiths at Jaén, Santarém and Badajoz; tanneries at Córdoba and Lleida; two levels of glassworks at Seville; a distillery at Jerez; a lumber mill at Écija; a winery at Castellón; a cannon foundry at Girona. Between February and March, potteries at Valencia, cloth and a tannery at Córdoba, and a furniture workshop at Cervera. The entry gives them to Aḥmad, as Trent asked for the earlier building.
- *The house:* Isabel de Montsoriu died of old age on 19 August 1447, her sixty-fifth birthday. Fāṭima was born on 29 June 1447 to Saʿd ibn Aḥmad of Banbalūna and Muʾmina, Prince Aḥmad's daughter. Aḥmad of Banbalūna is marked ruthless in October. The scholar of Delhi, the scholar of Iraq and the Tandroy lord (Matoky, of the Fahambahy) are new at court since February.
- *The war:* Tunis's war is the game's "2nd Tunis–Sicily War", fought for the province of Palermo, with a war goal granted by Tunis's parliament. The sides and the four battles are from the October save. Al-Andalus's own forces had not fought by then. In the Gulf of Termini the save records the Genoese attack as not carrying the day, but the allies lost 19 ships to Genoa's 1. The entry gives the losses and names no winner. Rodrigo de Borja, king of the Ṭāʾifa of Aragon, held a general's command from 2 July to 5 August 1447.
- *The explorer:* his commission ran from 30 October 1445 to 1 October 1447 in the save. Trent's account dates his sailing for the cape, to leave an outpost, to 5 October.

**From Trent's screenshots and account.** The Tīmūrid stagnation (27 March); the call from Tunis and "We are accepting" (13 June); "Presence in Europe" with the choice "We have arrived" and what it unlocks (1 August); the Umayyad Caliphate's borders; and Yūsuf raiding the coast of Naples, with the Accomplished Sailor report (13 January 1448).

**The Umayyad borders.** The game's term covers 2,816 locations: Iberia, the Maghrib (Sūs, Morocco, the Atlas plateau, Algiers, Tunis, Tripolitania and the desert areas), and the regions of Egypt, the Fertile Crescent, Arabia and Persia. In the October save:
- Iberia is al-Andalus and its ṭāʾifas, apart from five Moroccan locations and Andorra.
- The Maghrib is Morocco (with Algiers as its fiefdom and Touat as its vassal) and Tunis (with its tributaries and governorships), plus Fezzan and a few independents in the desert.
- Egypt and most of the Crescent are the Mamlūks'.
- Arabia is split among the Mamlūks, Mecca, Lām, Yemen and smaller lords.
- Persia is mostly the Tīmūrids' (350 locations).

So outside Persia and Arabia almost every claimable location belongs to an ally or an ally's subject. The entry leaves the court undecided, as Trent hasn't decided.

**Invented colour.**
- *Envoys entry:*
  - the news reaching the court by the Mamlūk post;
  - the Tabrīz merchants;
  - where the envoys came from;
  - the Emperor reading of ʿAbd al-Raḥmān II the night before, and enjoying the audience;
  - Ismāʿīl calling the answer a promise;
  - the jurists drawing up the claim;
  - the map in the dīwān;
  - Ismāʿīl's remark about the sword in the scabbard;
  - the Mamlūk party's objection;
  - Aḥmad studying the roads.
- *Isabel entry:*
  - the court's view of the birth;
  - Isabel coming south as a girl;
  - the Emperor's ceremony of mourning;
  - Aḥmad mourning by working, and his mother praising his drawings;
  - the purpose of the bridges;
  - the second bridge at al-Qanṭara (the Roman bridge at Alcántara is real history);
  - the pages following the Tandroy lord.
- *War entry:*
  - why Tunis wanted Palermo, beyond the parliament's war goal;
  - the dīwān's quick decision and Ismāʿīl's saying;
  - the sultan's letter asking for ships;
  - why Aḥmad of Banbalūna attacked, and his men's verdict;
  - Yūsuf's raids being quick;
  - the captains' words (the game's text is the chancellor's report and the trait);
  - the Emperor having the report read twice, and Ismāʿīl's remark.
