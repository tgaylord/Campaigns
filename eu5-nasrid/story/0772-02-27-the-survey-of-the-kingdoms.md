When the old map of the caliphs hung in the Hall of Ambassadors, our lord [Saʿd](person:saad-ibn-yusuf) saw that it showed only al-Andalus, and he asked for the rest of the world. He told the chancery to make him a survey of the kingdoms like the one Ibn Faḍl Allāh al-ʿUmarī wrote in Cairo a generation before, *The Roads of the Eyes through the Kingdoms of the Cities*. It was to say who ruled each kingdom, how many towns and men it had, whom it loved and whom it hated. The scribes questioned the Genoese factors in [Gharnāṭa](place:gharnata), the Mamlūk envoys, the captives of the last war and the pilgrims coming home from the East. At the end of Ṣafar 772 they laid before him a list of the powers of the world in order of their weight, and this is its substance.

**Of the far East.** The three heaviest kingdoms of the earth lie beyond India, in al-Ṣīn and the islands past it. The reckoners set them down with care, because the numbers were hard to believe: the first of them counts its people in tens of millions. None of them has ever heard of Gharnāṭa, and the sultan said it was a comfort to know that some empires are too far away to be a danger.

**Of Miṣr, the sultan's friend.** The seventh kingdom of the world is the realm of the Mamlūks, and it is the only crown that stands with Gharnāṭa. Its sultan is [Būlād](person:bolot-mamluk), who took the throne when his father Jamāl died in Ṣafar 770. He is twenty-six, and he is the half-brother of the sultan's wife [Aiman](person:aiman-mamluk), so the alliance now runs through the household as well as through the treaty. Miṣr has a hundred and fifty towns of its own and nearly as many again in its subjects, from the Ḥijāz to the Taurus, and some sixty thousand men under arms. Its weakness is at home: the envoys admitted that Cairo has been restless since the old sultan died.

**Of the house of ʿUthmān.** The Ottoman Turks have done what the Companions of the Prophet could not do, and taken Constantinople. Their sultan [Muḥammad](person:muhammad-ottoman) is twenty-two, the same age as our lord. He is a careful governor and a timid soldier, and the second is said of him more quietly. He is at war with Karaman and the beyliks of Anatolia, with twenty-seven thousand men in the field and seventy-three ships at sea. The Rūm who once ruled from Constantinople keep only twenty-five towns around Demetrias in Thessaly. The ʿulamāʾ wept when they heard it. A courtier pointed out that the Turks are twenty-seventh among the powers and nowhere near the Strait.

**Of the Franks.** France and England are the fourth and fifth kingdoms of the world, and they hate each other. [Jean of France](person:jean-of-france) has reigned for twenty-eight years and is losing his kingdom slowly. His realm is in disorder, and England's vassals hold Aquitaine, Burgundy, Flanders and Brittany. England has a new king, [Edward](person:edward-of-england), crowned in Dhū al-Ḥijja. His crown rules more land through its subjects than in England itself. The scribe noted that the king of France also counts Qashtāla among his enemies, and he wrote that line in red.

East of France sits [Charles of Bohemia](person:charles-of-bohemia), Emperor over three hundred and nineteen princes and cities. He is fifty-four, and by every report the ablest prince in Christendom. For reasons no envoy could explain, he names Fās and Tūnis among his rivals. The [Pope](person:pope-abundius), an old man of sixty, sits at Avignon rather than Rome, and of all the princes of the north he is the only friend of Qashtāla. Italy is divided between two leagues. The Guelphs follow Naples, the Pope and Genoa. The Ghibellines follow Florence, Venice, Milan and Sicily. Naples, Hungary and Croatia have one king, [Robert of Anjou](person:robert-of-anjou), a scholar of nineteen. His armies are far away in Halych and Volhynia. Genoa has only six towns of its own. But the scribe counted its trade and found it worth twice the trade of all Qashtāla, and he advised the sultan never to quarrel with a Genoese he owed money to.

**Of the Maghrib.** Fās is eleventh in the world, two places above Gharnāṭa. It is twice beaten but not broken. [Abū ʿInān Fāris](person:abu-inan-faris) rules Tlemcen as his fief, and Tlemcen still had eleven thousand men under arms when the peace was sealed. The Marīnid fleet is the true danger: eighty ships, against the sultan's four. The reckoners also found that Fās names Qashtāla, Portugal and Naples as its rivals, and not Gharnāṭa. The dīwān argued over whether this meant forgiveness or contempt. Beyond Fās, the Ḥafṣid [Ibrāhīm](person:abu-ishaq-ibrahim) rules Tūnis and nine subject lands, the twenty-first power of the world. His realm is close to disorder and his quarrel is with Tlemcen, so the Marīnids have an enemy at their back.

**Of the Land of the Blacks.** South of the sands, Mālī is sixteenth in the world, and every caravan of gold that reaches the Maghrib comes from its mines. Its throne stood empty when the scribes wrote, its heir is a boy of seven, and its armies are at war with the Mossi. Merchants said the gold still came north.

**Of the Peninsula.** Last the scribes came home. Qashtāla is eighth in the world, and with the Portuguese crown on its king's head since Ṣafar 769 it is the greatest power any sultan of Gharnāṭa has faced. But it is the last of the Great Powers but one, seven points above Bohemia, and no stronger. [Jerónimo](person:jeronimo-of-castile) kept no army in the field. [Arghūn](person:joan-of-aragon), forty-seventh in the world, has a king of twenty-one who also rules Sicily, and Arghūn itself has spent its strength.

**Of Gharnāṭa.** Then the scribes wrote the realm itself into the list: thirteenth among the powers of the world and a regional power, the equal of Delhi and of Majapahit in the islands of the Indies. Twenty years before, Gharnāṭa had no place on the list at all, because the reckoners only count sovereign kingdoms, and Gharnāṭa paid tribute to Qashtāla. Sixteen points now separate it from the last of the Great Powers.

The sultan read the whole survey in one sitting. Then he asked for three things to be copied out on a single leaf: the king of France is Qashtāla's enemy, Fās does not count Gharnāṭa among its enemies, and Miṣr is ruled by his wife's brother. He kept that leaf with him. The rest of the survey went to the treasury beside the map of the caliphs.

*O mankind, We have created you from male and female and made you peoples and tribes, that you may know one another.*
