The thirteen towns won from Arghūn were a gift that could choke the one who swallowed it. Nearly two hundred thousand souls lived in them, and three in four were Christians, Catalans and Aragonese who had never known a Muslim lord. The dīwān of Gharnāṭa had neither the clerks nor the soldiers to govern them from the Alhambra. The sultan's counsellors remembered how the old caliphate had fallen. The great city had tried to hold everything, and in the end it held nothing.

So our lord [Yūsuf](person:yusuf-i) chose the way of the old kings of al-Andalus, before the caliphs and after them. On the twenty-eighth of Ṣafar in the year 741 he raised two ṭāʾifas, two lesser kingdoms bound to Gharnāṭa by oath and tribute and ruled by their own princes. The first was the Ṭāʾifa of [Balansiyya](place:balansiyya), with the great city and its twelve towns, from [Shāṭiba](place:shatiba) to [Uryūla](place:uryula). Over it he set [Joan d'Oriola](person:joan-doriola), a Catalan lord of that land who had taken the faith of Islam. The second was the Ṭāʾifa of Mursiyya, which for now was only the one town of [Jumalla](place:jumalla). Over it he set [Juan Manuel Enríquez](person:juan-manuel-enriquez), a Castilian and likewise a convert, a hard soldier with no gift for words. Both princes kept the Christian names they were born with, for their people knew them by those names and would follow them more readily.

Neither ṭāʾifa loved its new master. The prince of Balansiyya was near content and growing more so. The prince of Mursiyya was a Castilian, and his loyalty was thin. The sultan's envoys went to both with gifts and assurances, and the dīwān settled down to the long work of making friends of them.

At home the realm put down its arms. The host of nineteen regiments was sent back to its villages until three remained. Five of the loans owed to the estates were repaid from the gold of the peace, and the debt fell from nine hundred and sixty-four dīnārs to eight hundred and forty-three. The treasury was left almost bare again. The sultan's cousin [Yūsuf ibn Muḥammad](person:yusuf-ibn-muhammad), who had spent three years defending the realm's sovereignty, was turned to strengthening the government. Within days of the peace the clerks of the dīwān began, very quietly, to set down the realm's claims against Qashtāla, the long grievance of tribute and lost lands that would one day justify a war of independence. No one spoke of it aloud. The scales were not ready.

*God does not change the condition of a people until they change what is in themselves.*
