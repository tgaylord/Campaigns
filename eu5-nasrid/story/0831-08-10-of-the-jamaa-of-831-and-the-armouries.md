The petty kings of the mountains did not stay four for long. In Jumādā al-Ūlā 828 [Diego](person:diego-villamarin) of Galicia, the soldier king, made war on Asturias and took all of it, Oviedo and its eight towns, and Galicia stretched from Santiago to Llanes. Three Christian kingdoms were left in the north: Galicia, Navarre on the Basque coast, and what remained of Qashtāla at Santander. In Rabīʿ al-Awwal 829 the truce of the Peace of Rajab ran out, and for the first time in fifteen years nothing bound the empire to peace with any of them. A year later the English went to war with France again, and King Fernando of Qashtāla went with them, until in Jumādā al-Ūlā 831 he made a peace of his own with the French and came home.

The Emperor [Ismāʿīl](person:ismail-ibn-saad) was sixty-two. He had never been a soldier, and he had always known what he wanted. He wanted the last war of the Istirdād fought in his own lifetime, and he set about it as he had set about everything, by making sure that when it came it would be short. In the spring of 831 his spies went north into all three kingdoms. They did not go to count soldiers. They went to talk, in the markets and the monasteries and the lords' halls, about the return of al-Andalus: that it was not a conquest but a homecoming, that the Portuguese and the Catalans now sat in the Emperor's dīwān, and that every Iberian, whatever he prayed, had a place in it. The secretaries who wrote their instructions remembered what the Emperor had said after Fermoselle, that four small kingdoms were easier to take one at a time, and they noticed that the spies were told to say something a little different in each.

At home he began to build an army that would not need the umarāʾ to raise it. In a dozen towns and cities of the empire, from Lisbon, Évora and Santarém to Toledo, Córdoba, Granada, Málaga and Almería, the crown built armouries, so that each could arm its own men and fill the ranks without waiting on any lord's levy. The umarāʾ watched the walls go up and understood exactly what they were for.

The house changed in those years too. In Ṣafar 829 the heir's daughter [Umm al-Fatḥ](person:umm-al-fath-bint-muhammad), fourteen years old and named for her grandmother, took a seat in the dīwān beside her mother [Jadīla](person:jadila-bint-saad-ibn-faraj), and the clerks said she was already abler than her father had ever been. Saʿd ibn Yūsuf and then Prince Yūsuf himself turned from settling the new lands to spreading the tongue and learning of al-Andalus through them. And on 15 Rajab 831 [Yūsuf ibn Ismāʿīl](person:yusuf-ibn-ismail), lord of [Banbalūna](place:banbaluna) since the Peace of Rajab, died of a fever in camp at fifty-six. His son [Aḥmad](person:ahmad-ibn-yusuf-ibn-ismail), who had sat in the dīwān for thirteen years and was one of the ablest men of the house, succeeded him on the Navarrese frontier.

Then the Emperor called the Jamāʿa. It met at [Ishbīliya](place:ishbiliyya) on 10 Shaʿbān 831, and the Emperor told it that a war was coming and the empire must be ready. He asked the assembly for one thing. Since the days of the first sultans the great houses had held the Primacy of Nobility: they counted themselves the ruler's equals, and no important decision could be made without them. The Emperor said that an empire about to go to war could not wait for every lord in it to be consulted, and that the caliphs of Qurṭuba had consulted no one. The ʿulamāʾ, the merchants and the dhimmī voted with the crown, and the Primacy was revoked.

The umarāʾ had lost their mercenaries in 817 and their lordships with them. Now they had lost their equality. Barely one great house in six was content with the crown, and the peace of the realm, which had been mended slowly for fourteen years, was worn through. The Emperor did not send them gifts this time. He had armouries now, and a war to fight, and he seemed to think the umarāʾ would come round once they had castles in Galicia to quarrel over.

*Indeed, Allāh commands you to render trusts to whom they are due, and when you judge between people, to judge with justice.*
