The winter brought our allies nothing but grief. In the bay of Palma the Aragonese galleys sank the fleets of Bijāya and [Tilimsān](place:tilimsan), fourteen ships sent to the bottom without one Christian hull lost. Off Alcúdia they did the same to the fleet of Qābis. The ships of [Balansiyya](place:balansiyya) were worsted by the Portuguese in the very gulf of [al-Mariyya](place:al-mariyya), within sight of our own shore. On the first of Dhū al-Qaʿda the Ṭāʾifa's little army of two thousand was caught at al-Qasṭalyūn by eight thousand Aragonese and destroyed to the last man. And on the third of Dhū al-Ḥijja the Marīnids, landed at last on our side of the Strait, came before [Ishbīliya](place:ishbiliyya) with fewer than three thousand men. The Castilians met them with nearly twelve thousand and drove them off.

The Christians took heart and believed the Muslims of the West could be beaten one by one. The tyrant himself, [Alfonso](person:alfonso-xi), son of Ferdinand, king of Qashtāla, came south with thirteen thousand men to finish the matter before the walls of [Jayyān](place:jayyan).

Our lord [Yūsuf](person:yusuf-i) had marched north to meet him with twenty-two thousand of the faithful, and three and a half thousand Moroccans joined him on the road. It was the sixth of Ṣafar in the year 748. The king of Qashtāla was a soldier who had fought Muslims since his youth. He did not refuse battle, though he was outnumbered two to one, for he thought the Andalusīs soft from long peace and fat on Catalan trade. The Volunteers held the centre with the *mutaṭawwiʿa* in the first line, as at Uryūla. The jinetes rode on the wings in the old manner of al-Andalus, wheeling in, loosing and wheeling out again. All day the Castilian knights threw themselves at the line and all day the line held. At evening the jinetes turned their flank, and the Castilian army broke.

Seven thousand five hundred Castilians fell or were taken, more than half of those who came. We lost four thousand two hundred, Allāh have mercy on them. That night the sultan did not sit down to the victory feast. He went among the wounded with the physicians and a lamp, cleaning and binding wounds with his own hands as he had learned to do on campaign, and he had the Castilian wounded carried in and laid beside ours. The physicians said he had a surgeon's hands and wasted them on a throne. The king escaped with his household, but he left the confidence of the winter on that field. The Andalusīs, whom the Christians had called soft, began to call themselves the sword of the West.

*Truth has come, and falsehood has departed. Indeed, falsehood is ever bound to depart.*
