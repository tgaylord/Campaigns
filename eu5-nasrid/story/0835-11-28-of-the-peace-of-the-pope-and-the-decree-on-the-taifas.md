By the summer of 835 the crusade had run out of crusaders. The Ḥafṣid landings in Lazio ended at Terracina, where the Pope's army caught two hundred and seventy Tunisians and killed them all, and the Moroccans who had marched on Viterbo were beaten off by an army seven times their size. But the Pope's army could not stay in Lazio. Since Rajab the lords of Mantua and Milan had been at war with Faenza, his vassal, and the Pope, Genoa and every subject of Genoa were bound to defend it. The court of [Ishbīliya](place:ishbiliyya) watched the princes of Christendom fight a crusade against al-Andalus with one hand and each other with the other, and found it very satisfying.

The war at sea ended in the Ocean, where it had never been expected. On 27 Shawwāl a fleet of twenty-nine ships from Mecklenburg, on the Baltic, came down the coast of Burtuqāl and attacked a fleet off Cape Mondego. It was [Berenguer Ramon](person:berenguer-ramon-de-montsoriu) of Catalonia with thirty-two Catalan ships and eight of the Emperor's own. The Mecklenburgers lost eighteen ships and did not sink one. It was the first time in the war that the Emperor's ships had fought, and the Emperor had the report read to him more than once.

In Dhū al-Qaʿda the Pope's envoys came to Ishbīliya asking for peace. The Emperor's terms were short. The Pope would pay a hundred and fifteen dīnārs at once and a tenth of his income for five years, some thirty-six dīnārs a month, in reparation for the war he had begun, and he would annul every treaty he had with Genoa, Siena and Aquileia, so that the next pope who preached a crusade would have to find his allies again from the beginning. No town changed hands. [Fabrizio Ricci](person:pope-fabrizio-ricci) accepted on 28 Dhū al-Qaʿda 835 (27 July 1432), and the Crusade of 835 was over ten months after it began. The court called it the Peace of Dhū al-Qaʿda. It said that the Christians could not even keep their own quarrels in check long enough to fight the Muslims.

The peace changed the Emperor. His father and grandfather had left the ṭāʾifas to rule their own courts in their own way, Christian subjects and all, and had been content with tribute, loyalty and soldiers. Muḥammad was no longer content. A crusade had been preached against his realm and fought in the name of its Christians, and he did not mean to leave enemies in his rear for the next one. He decreed that the ṭāʾifas of Arghūn, Qūnka, Banbalūna, Burghush and Liyūn, each as soon as it was bound closely enough to the crown, were to impose the ways of al-Andalus on the Christians who remained in their lands.

Only two were bound closely enough yet, and both were ruled by the Emperor's own family by marriage. Arghūn belonged to [Rodrigo de Borja](person:rodrigo-de-borja), the brother of the Empress [Magdalena](person:magdalena-de-borja), a gentle and talkative man whose kingdom had just been the target of the crusade. Qūnka belonged to the Shaykha [Maryam](person:maryam-bint-saad-ibn-faraj), sister of the Empress [Jadīla](person:jadila-bint-saad-ibn-faraj) and married to the Emperor's brother [Saʿd](person:saad-ibn-ismail-ibn-saad). Their courts were ordered to act, and they obeyed. They also complained, and in the ṭāʾifas of the north, which knew their turn would come, the complaints were louder. For three generations the house of Naṣr had told the Christians of Iberia that its coming was a homecoming. Some of the old men of the dīwān wondered what the Christians would call it now.

*And had your Lord willed, those on earth would have believed, all of them entirely. Then will you compel the people in order that they become believers?*
