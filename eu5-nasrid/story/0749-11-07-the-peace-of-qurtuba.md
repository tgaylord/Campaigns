In Jumādā al-Ākhira of the year 749 the first crown fell away. Portugal had lost no army and hardly a ship, but fifty-five thousand of its people had died in the years of the war. It made a separate peace with the Marīnids and annulled for ten years every treaty it had with Qashtāla. It paid reparations and gave a hundred and forty gold pieces to the allies besides. [Alfonso](person:alfonso-xi) of Qashtāla was left with Arghūn alone beside him, and Arghūn's best fleet lay at the bottom of the sea off Cape Ben Sakka.

The pestilence did not take sides. It came into Qashtāla as it had come into [Gharnāṭa](place:gharnata), and it found the Castilian armies in their winter camps, crowded, hungry and beaten. The levies deserted to bury their families. The captains wrote to the king that they could not hold the frontier. And at the beginning of Dhū al-Qaʿda in the year 749 the envoys of Alfonso came, not to the Marīnid sultan, who had begun the war, but to our lord [Yūsuf](person:yusuf-i), who had won it. They asked for a separate peace with Gharnāṭa alone.

The sultan received the envoys standing, with the scar of al-Manṣūra still red across his face. He named his terms once, town by town, in a level voice, and when the Castilians began to bargain he left them with his secretary and went out to his other business. The dīwān expected the terms to be refused. Before evening the envoys had sent after him. On the seventh of Dhū al-Qaʿda the Peace of [Qurṭuba](place:qurtuba) was sealed. Qashtāla gave up twenty-three towns along the river and in the hills of the frontier:

- Qurṭuba of the caliphs, lost a hundred and twelve years before;
- [Ishbīliya](place:ishbiliyya), lost a hundred years before;
- [Jayyān](place:jayyan), where the tyrant had been broken;
- Ubbadha al-ʿArab, Bayyāsa and [Istijja](place:istijja), and al-Yusāna, Martush and Ūshūna;
- [al-Quṣayr](place:al-qusayr) and [Balma](place:balma), where the sultan had won his victories;
- [Wubīra](place:wubira) of the old Naṣrid claim, and others besides.

The Ṭāʾifa of [Mursiyya](place:mursiyya) took the whole of its own land, eleven towns more. Qashtāla paid reparations in gold and annulled its treaties with Arghūn. In return Gharnāṭa took again the name of Qashtāla's tributary.

It was only a name. The realm that had held nineteen of the seventy-two towns of the Strait's three lands now held fifty-three. It had been eighteen towns and was now forty-one. Its souls had been three hundred and seventy-seven thousand, after the plague's first year, and were now seven hundred and fifty-four thousand. But nearly half of them were Castilian Christians who had gone to sleep subjects of Alfonso and woken subjects of the house of Naṣr. They had not been asked, and they had buried their own dead that winter.

The realm was greater than at any time since the house of Naṣr was founded, and it was not at peace with itself. War and plague had shaken every estate, and the realm was more unsettled than at any time in the reign. In the new lands the bells still rang for the Christian dead. The Marīnids fought on alone. The sultan had made the peace his victories had earned, and Fās would have to make its own.

*And if they incline to peace, then incline to it also, and rely upon Allāh.*
