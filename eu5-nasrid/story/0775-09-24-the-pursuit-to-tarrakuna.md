Our lord [Saʿd](person:saad-ibn-yusuf) gave the Catalans no time to gather again under the walls of [Barshalūna](place:barshaluna). He marched the whole army south along the coast after what was left of King [Joan](person:joan-of-aragon)'s army: thirteen hundred archers and a few dozen riders making for the old Roman walls of [Ṭarrakūna](place:tarrakuna).

The jinetes caught them on the plain before the city, and the foot came up behind; it ended on 24 Ramaḍān. It was hardly a battle. The archers had no horse to cover them and nowhere to run, and the sultan's riders broke them in a morning. Thirteen hundred Catalans were killed. Eleven horsemen surrendered and were taken into the sultan's service, to the delight of the court, which said that Arghūn was now paying the sultan in men. Thirty-one of ours were lost.

When the sultan broke the fast that night in the camp outside Ṭarrakūna, the scouts reported that the whole host of Arghūn now came to a hundred and sixty-one levies hiding in the mountains. The king who wore two crowns had no army left to defend either of them.

The sultan's council sat late that night, and the talk was of Ifranja as much as of Arghūn. Beyond the Pyrenees the king of France, [Jean](person:jean-of-france), was fighting for his kingdom against the English again. The merchants said the English held the west of France from the sea of Normandy to Poitou, and that every French lance was needed there. In any other year, the sultan said, he would have been wary of carrying his banners up to the French border, because a French king with nothing else to do might come over the mountains to save his Catalan neighbour. This year the French king had plenty to do. Every month the English kept him busy was a month the Upper March lay open. The sultan resolved to take all of Arghūn, from the Ebro to the Pyrenees, before France was free to look south. He told the dīwān it was a race, and that he meant to win it.

Before he slept he sealed the week's letters for home, as he did throughout the campaign, and the war took up less of them than the building. The treasury was to go on paying for the new royal court rising in [Gharnāṭa](place:gharnata), for the mines being cut at Almonaster in the hills above [Walba](place:walba), and for stronger walls on the frontier fort at Aracena, on the Castilian border. The Upper March, he wrote, was no reason to leave the Lower March unguarded, and a mine opened in a year of war paid as well as one opened in peace.

*So they defeated them by permission of Allāh.*
