The Emperor [Muḥammad](person:muhammad-ibn-ismail-ibn-saad) had been told all his life that he was not the man his forebears were. His grandfather [Saʿd](person:saad-ibn-yusuf) had restored al-Andalus and his father [Ismāʿīl](person:ismail-ibn-saad) had built it a madrasa in every town, and the court had judged him unfit at twelve and said so again when he took the throne. He had never argued. He had gone to sea instead, and then to Makka, and only two years before, when the old men hailed the age of the caliphs come again, he had accepted the compliment for his forebears and gone back to his ships. Then, in the year 842, past fifty, he did something no one at court had expected of him: he began to read.

He read the way he did everything, slowly and without shame. He had the pilots of the Ocean voyages brought to him to explain their charts, and kept them until he understood the charts himself. He sat in on the lectures of the madrasa of Ishbīliya, at the back, and asked the questions the students were too proud to ask. He had the astronomers explain the instruments by which a ship found its latitude, and the physicians explain the plagues that came in with the ships, and the engineers of the arsenals explain why one hull was faster than another. Within the year, by Ṣafar 843, the scholars of the court had stopped humouring him and begun to argue with him, and they agreed among themselves that he understood a new idea, once he had it, better and faster than most of them. Under him the realm took up new learning more readily than it ever had, and the madrasas taught more children to read.

He did not pretend to be his father. He said that his father had known things and he had to find them out, and that the finding out was the part he enjoyed. The court noticed that he had begun to say "the old Emperors" with less weight in his voice than before.

It was a hard year at home. The umarāʾ had not taken the loss of their land rights quietly, the stipend for their poets notwithstanding, and the peace of the realm had fallen further in a year than in any year since the risings of 807. The princes [Ismāʿīl](person:ismail-ibn-muhammad-ibn-ismail) and [Aḥmad](person:ahmad-ibn-muhammad-ibn-ismail) spent it appeasing the estates while the charter of [Majrīṭ](place:majrit) worked its way through the chancery. The Emperor left that to them and went back to his books.

*Say: Are those who know equal to those who do not know? Only they will remember who are people of understanding.*
