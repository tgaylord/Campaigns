The chroniclers of other courts write their wars from the field. This one must also be written from the harem, because in the autumn of 770 the war with [Fās](place:fas) was fought there too.

When the Marīnid sultan [Abū ʿInān](person:abu-inan-faris) declared his second war for the submission of Gharnāṭa, the first question at court was not where he would land. It was what [Duʿāʾ](person:dua-marinid) would do. She was a daughter of his house. She sat in the dīwān, she had borne the heir, and she had made half the court read Maghribī books and eat Maghribī food. Now the Maghrib was coming across the Strait with an army. In the souks they said she had letters from Fās. In the harem they said worse. The whisperers never found the letters, because there were none, but they did not need them.

[Aiman](person:aiman-mamluk)'s party did not need to whisper. Two years before, when Cairo had let its alliance with Gharnāṭa lapse, it was Aiman who had written to Cairo, and it was her envoy who brought the treaty home in Jumādā al-Ūlā 768. Egypt stood with Gharnāṭa again because of her. Now her people said it openly in the dīwān: the Mamlūks were the realm's friends, the Marīnids its enemies, and the sultan should consider which queen came from which.

Duʿāʾ answered as she answered everything, with work. She gave her jewels to the war chest, in public, before the dīwān. She had the Maghribī scholars of her circle preach the jihad against the Marīnid in the mosques of [Gharnāṭa](place:gharnata), which they did with more fervour than the Andalusīs. She sent her son [Ismāʿīl](person:ismail-ibn-saad), who was two, to be shown to the troops at the gate. The court said she had become more Andalusī than the Andalusīs. Some of them meant it as praise.

Between the two queens stood [Sayyida](person:sayyida-of-fezzan), the sultan's mother. She had never forgiven Duʿāʾ for marrying her husband and then her son. She did not join the whisperers. She said, in the hearing of the whole court, that the sultan's wives were the sultan's business, and that a court that talked about them in wartime had too little to do. The whispering grew quieter after that. The court understood that the matriarch had not defended Duʿāʾ. She had defended the house.

The war itself settled the question. On 29 Rabīʿ al-Awwal [Murād](person:murad) broke Abū ʿInān's army before [Qādis](place:qadis), and the Marīnid sultan went home. No letter from Fās was ever found. Our lord [Saʿd](person:saad-ibn-yusuf), who had said nothing through all of it, said one thing afterwards: that he had married the Maghrib and Egypt both, and meant to keep them both. He kept them both. He did not reconcile them.

*And if you fear dissension between the two, send an arbitrator from his people and an arbitrator from her people.*
