On 25 Muḥarram 819 [Umm al-Fatḥ](person:umm-al-fath-bint-saad), the old Emperor's first child, died at fifty-one. She had sat in the dīwān for twenty-two years, from the year of the League, and for most of them she had done one thing: she took lands the armies had won and made them part of the empire, from the valley of the Tagus to the country of the risings. Her seat stood empty for most of two years. Her daughter [Jadīla](person:jadila-bint-saad-ibn-faraj), the heir's third wife, sat in the dīwān already, and in Shaʿbān the Emperor took her off the settling of migrants and gave her the hardest task he had, to keep the realm at peace. The umarāʾ were still sore from the Jamāʿa of 817, and Prince [Yūsuf](person:yusuf-ibn-saad) was still at work soothing them.

On the first day of Ramaḍān 819 the Emperor [Ismāʿīl](person:ismail-ibn-saad) told the dīwān how the empire would be governed from then on. The state was the Emperor's, and those who served it served him. The viziers and the governors were members of his household, as the slaves of the palace and the eunuchs of the harem were, and they would answer to him as his household answered, without appeal to any estate. The ʿulamāʾ looked for a precedent and found one in the caliphs of Qurṭuba, who had governed through the men of the palace and not through the great families. The umarāʾ looked for a way to object and found none. They had already given up their mercenaries and their lordships, and they had nothing left to give up but their pride.

He used his new hand at once. On 27 Ramaḍān, the night the faithful keep as the Night of Power, sixteen of the twenty-one towns of the old Ṭāʾifa of [Shalamanka](place:shalamanka) were given away. The crown had taken them in a year and a half before, when the ṭāʾifa's lord died, and the Emperor had never wanted them. Shalamanka itself, Ciudad Rodrigo, Ledesma, Béjar and Coria and five more went to his son [Aḥmad](person:ahmad-ibn-ismail-ibn-saad) in Liyūn, and Ávila, Arévalo, Oropesa and three more went to [Abū Ḥammū](person:abu-hammu-rahhu) in Burghush. The rule his father had begun was now the rule of the empire. The north, where the Christians were many and the Muslims few, would be held through lords who owed their thrones to the Emperor, and the lands south of it would be the crown's. [Aḥmad ibn Yūsuf](person:ahmad-ibn-yusuf-ibn-ismail) of the dīwān was given new lands to integrate that same day, and in Dhū al-Qaʿda 820 the Emperor's nephew [Saʿd ibn Yūsuf](person:saad-ibn-yusuf-ibn-saad), raised at his court since the age of four, took Umm al-Fatḥ's seat.

The generation of Yūsuf I passed in those years. [Jadīla](person:jadila-bint-yusuf), his daughter, queen of the Marīnids for thirty-five years, died in Dhū al-Qaʿda 819, three months before her husband. [ʿĀʾisha](person:aisha-bint-yusuf), his last child, died in Shaʿbān 822. The court mourned them as the last of the sultanate, the realm of the old Alhambra that had paid tribute to Qashtāla. The empire that buried them stretched from Porto to Barshalūna.

The next generation had grown up meanwhile. The heir [Muḥammad](person:muhammad-ibn-ismail-ibn-saad) was thirty-four and no better a governor than he had been at seventeen, and the clerks of the treasury now complained that he squeezed his own estates for every dirham they had. But his sons were men. [Ismāʿīl](person:ismail-ibn-muhammad-ibn-ismail) and [Aḥmad](person:ahmad-ibn-muhammad-ibn-ismail), born eight months apart, had come out of their tutors' hands as able as anyone in the house, and the younger, Isabel's son, was better at every art than the elder. No one at court said aloud that the crown would pass from a strong man through a weak one to a strong one again. Everyone had worked it out.

On 21 Rajab 823 [Balansiyya](place:balansiyya) came into the crown. It had been a ṭāʾifa since Ṣafar 741, when the young Yūsuf I had won it from Arghūn and given it to Joan d'Oriola to hold for him: seventeen towns, from Balansiyya itself, [Shāṭiba](place:shatiba) and [Dāniya](place:daniya) to [al-Qant](place:al-qant), Elche and Orihuela, and two hundred and twenty thousand people, Catalan in speech and Muslim in faith. Its last lord, Melchior, of Joan d'Oriola's house, went to the court of Catalonia with his family and his household. No one in Balansiyya had been asked, and no one in Balansiyya complained. The Emperor's letters had been preparing them for three years, and the governor who would sit in the city had a university and a great road waiting for him.

The Emperor did not stay for the ceremony. On 19 Jumādā al-Ūlā the Castilians of Zamora had risen against his son in Liyūn, under a lord named [Eduardo de Avellaneda](person:eduardo-de-avellaneda), and proclaimed a kingdom of their own. It was a kingdom of a few hundred men. The Emperor was fifty-four and had not commanded an army since the War of Jillīqiya, and he took the whole standing army of the empire north himself, four thousand nine hundred men, and found the rebels at Fermoselle above the gorges of the Duero. On 30 Rajab he destroyed them, all three hundred and seventy, and lost no one. The next day was the first of Shaʿbān, the rising was over, and the Emperor's letters to the ṭāʾifas of the north had already gone out. A lord who could not keep his own towns quiet, they said, would find the Emperor there to keep them for him, and the Emperor never left a town quite as he had found it.

*Blessed is He in whose hand is dominion, and He is over all things competent.*
