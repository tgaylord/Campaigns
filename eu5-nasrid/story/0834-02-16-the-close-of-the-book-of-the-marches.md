Here ends the Book of the Marches.

It opened sixty-two years ago by the reckoning of the Hijra, on 21 Ṣafar 772, on the day the Marīnids gave up the north shore and the throne of [Gharnāṭa](place:gharnata) was secure at last. The Emir [Saʿd](person:saad-ibn-yusuf) then ruled sixty towns and three quarters of a million people from the Alhambra. The old thughūr of al-Andalus, the Lower March of Badajoz, the Middle March of Toledo and the Upper March of Zaragoza, had been Christian country for two and three centuries, and Qashtāla was the greatest kingdom of the peninsula. The ʿulamāʾ of that day prayed for the restoration of al-Andalus as men pray for rain in a drought, without much expecting it.

Six wars brought it. The War of the Upper March broke Aragon and brought Barshalūna and Catalonia under the crown, and on 16 March 1375 Saʿd proclaimed the restoration of al-Andalus and took the title of Emperor. The War of the Aragonese Restoration, the War of the League and the War of Jillīqiya broke Qashtāla piece by piece, ended Portugal, and drove the Castilians back into the mountains of the north. The War of Shant Yāqub and the War of the Mountains finished them. Three Emperors fought those wars. Saʿd won the marches, his son [Ismāʿīl](person:ismail-ibn-saad) the heartland of Old Castile, and his grandson [Muḥammad](person:muhammad-ibn-ismail-ibn-saad) the mountains.

The Christians had always said that their reconquest began in those mountains, when a Gothic lord named Pelayo held a cave at Covadonga against the first governors of al-Andalus, above the town the Asturians call Cangas. On 16 Ṣafar 834 Cangas was given with the rest of Ashturiyās to the Emperor's brother [Aḥmad](person:ahmad-ibn-ismail-ibn-saad) of Liyūn, a prince of the house of Naṣr. In 814 the ʿulamāʾ had said that one more war would push Qashtāla into the sea. Its last king rode out of Santander to live as a guest in Burghush, and the sea he left behind him was the empire's.

The empire that closes the book has four hundred and thirty-two towns, the crown's and the ṭāʾifas' together, and six and a half million people, from the Bay of Biscay to the Green Cape. There are no marches left in Iberia, only borders: with the English along the Pyrenees, and the open sea everywhere else.

The court has not forgotten Shant Yāqub. In [Ishbīliya](place:ishbiliyya) the Pórtico de la Gloria waits in a storeroom for the Emperor to decide where to set it up, and every merchant ship from the north brings word of what the Christians are saying about the Apostle's tomb. In 387 al-Manṣūr carried the bells of Shant Yāqub to [Qurṭuba](place:qurtuba) and left the tomb untouched. The Christians took Qurṭuba back two and a half centuries later, and they carried the bells back to Shant Yāqub on Muslim shoulders. The princes [Ismāʿīl](person:ismail-ibn-muhammad-ibn-ismail) and [Aḥmad](person:ahmad-ibn-muhammad-ibn-ismail) know that story, as every child of the house does. Their father looks at the sea.

*And We have already written in the Book, after the Reminder, that the land is inherited by My righteous servants.*
