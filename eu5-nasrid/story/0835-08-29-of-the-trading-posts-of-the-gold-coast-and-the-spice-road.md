A crusade was being fought in the waters of Sardinia, and the Emperor [Muḥammad](person:muhammad-ibn-ismail-ibn-saad) was thinking about the rivers of Guinea. His father [Ismāʿīl](person:ismail-ibn-saad) had sent envoys to Niani and won the Mansa's word that Andalusī merchants would pay no tariff anywhere in Mālī. The son meant to use it. In the spring of 835 the crown began eight trading posts on the coast of Kaabu, the Mansa's vassal on the Gambia and the rivers south of it: at the mouth of the Gambia, at Bintang, on the Casamance and at Diaboudior, at Cacheu and Bissau, and at Bafatá and Tankular. They were stores and wharves more than towns, where the ships that watered at the islands of the Green Cape could load the gold that came down the rivers from the mines of the interior without sending a single caravan across the Ṣaḥrāʾ.

The caravans were not forgotten either. The empire already kept trade offices at Niani and at Timbuktu, at the ports of Ifrīqiya and Sicily, and in the cities of the Marīnids. Now the offices at Marrakesh and at Sijilmasa, where the gold of the desert road came out of the sands, were ordered enlarged again and again, and a new office was opened at Cairo, beside the Emperor's embassy there, with a plan for its growth that ran to more than twenty stages. Cairo was where the pepper, cloves and cinnamon of the Indies came up the Red Sea from the ports of Yemen, and the Emperor wanted the spices of the East to reach Ishbīliya through the hands of his own merchants and those of his Mamlūk allies, with no Venetian or Genoese in between.

The treasurers asked whether this was the time. The fleet had seven arsenals to pay for, and the crusade was not over. The Emperor said that the war would end and the trade would not, and that the Genoese were at war with him for the same reason he was building in Cairo. The empire still kept a trade office in Genoa itself, and he gave no order to close it.

*For the accustomed security of the Quraysh, their accustomed security in the caravan of winter and of summer, let them worship the Lord of this House, who has fed them against hunger and made them safe from fear.*
