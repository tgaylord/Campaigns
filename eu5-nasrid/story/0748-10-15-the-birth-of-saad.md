In the middle of Shawwāl in the year 748, while the host wintered on the frontier of Qurṭuba, a rider came up from [Gharnāṭa](place:gharnata) with the news the court had waited for. The lady [Sayyida](person:sayyida-of-fezzan), daughter of the house of Murzuq in Fazzān, had given our lord [Yūsuf](person:yusuf-i) a son. He was named [Saʿd](person:saad-ibn-yusuf), which is good fortune, and the name was thought well chosen: he was born in the second year of the war, in a season of victories, with the pestilence still far off in the East.

Sayyida had come to Gharnāṭa a child of nine, betrothed to the young sultan in 738, and had married him in the year of the Jamāʿa. The women of the palace had long whispered that the harem the Jamāʿa enshrined would fill with sons. Now it had given one to the house of Naṣr. Alms were given at the gates of the Alhambra, and the poets of the court wrote odes that were better in their sentiment than their metre.

That same day a Naṣrid betrothal was sealed with the Marīnids of Fās, binding the two houses as the war bound the two realms. The dīwān thought it a prudent knot. In time the court would learn how quickly the knots Morocco tied could come undone, and that the brides worth having for Saʿd lay further east.

*Wealth and children are the adornment of the life of this world.*
