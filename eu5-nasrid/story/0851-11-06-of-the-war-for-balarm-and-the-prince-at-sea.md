On 28 Rabīʿ al-Awwal 851 the Ḥafṣid sultan of Tūnis declared war on the king of Ṣiqilliya for Balarm and its province. Balarm had been a city of the faith for more than two centuries before the Normans took it, and the assembly of Tūnis had voted for the war. The same day the sultan's letter came to [Ishbīliya](place:ishbiliyya) calling the Emperor to arms.

The dīwān did not take long over it. Sicily had Athens and Negroponte behind it, and its allies Genoa and the Pope would come in, Genoa bringing Arborea, Corsica and the rest of its little subjects. The heir [Ismāʿīl](person:ismail-ibn-muhammad-ibn-ismail) said what the court had said of every such call since his grandfather's time: an alliance that is not answered is not an alliance. The Emperor [Muḥammad](person:muhammad-ibn-ismail-ibn-saad) accepted, and the seven ṭāʾifas came in with him that day. The old king of Aragon, [Rodrigo de Borja](person:rodrigo-de-borja), was Ismāʿīl's uncle and sixty-six years old. He took the field himself in the early summer and gave up his command a month later.

The summer went badly for the allies. Al-Andalus itself had not yet fought. On 23 Jumādā al-Ūlā, at Ozieri in Sardinia, Arborea's four thousand broke the Gallurese, Tūnis's vassals, who lost half their men. On 27 Jumādā al-Ākhira two Sicilian ships drove three of al-Burtughāl's off the western shore of Corsica. Two days later, in the gulf of Termini, a day's sail from Balarm, six Genoese ships fell on the combined fleet of Tūnis, Ṭarābulus and Qābis, twenty-eight sail. Nineteen were lost, to one of Genoa's. The Ḥafṣid sultan wrote to Ishbīliya that the Genoese were few and the sea was wide, and asked for ships.

On 24 Rajab [Aḥmad](person:ahmad-ibn-yusuf-ibn-ismail), lord of Banbalūna, who was fifty-one years old, found four thousand two hundred Genoese at Grasse in the hills of Provence. He had three hundred and eighty-five men, and he attacked anyway. Twenty-nine came back. He did not explain himself to the dīwān, and the men of Banbalūna said afterwards that their lord had grown hard.

The Emperor's grandson [Yūsuf](person:yusuf-ibn-ismail-ibn-muhammad) was the one who answered the sultan. He had been admiral since he was sixteen, made so by a grandfather who believed the sea had made a man of him. In the winter, at twenty-six, he took the eleventh squadron out and raided along the coasts of Nābulī. His galleys were in and out again before the coast guards had their sails up. On 6 Dhū al-Qaʿda 851 the chancellor reported to the Emperor that the captains of the squadron called the prince an accomplished sailor, and that ships under him reached where they were going sooner than ships under any other man.

The Emperor had the report read to him twice. His son Ismāʿīl said only that Yūsuf had his grandfather's love of the sea and, he hoped, his father's judgement of when to leave it.

*And do not weaken in pursuit of the enemy. If you should be suffering, so are they suffering as you are suffering, but you expect from God that which they expect not. And God is ever Knowing and Wise.*
