The second survey follows the game's great-power ladder in the save of 11 August 1420, as the first did in 1370. Trent's reading: the East has formed into three empires. The ladder agrees. Tīmūr (fourth, 707 towns), the Ottomans (sixth, 294) and the Mamlūks (ninth, 191 of their own and 103 in vassals) are the only Great Powers of the East on it, and the Tīmūrids now join the Mamlūks and the Ottomans on the Mashriq circle of the Kingdoms hall. The Ottoman peace of 22 April 1417 and the Tīmūrid conquests of 1414–1415 are counted from the towns that changed hands in the save, not from any treaty text. "Men under arms" is still regulars and raised levies on the map. A zero for Castile means no army in the field, not none to raise.

Rulers' character comes from their game traits: Tīmūr a conqueror, tactical genius, strategist and cruel; Aḥmad a zealot of 17/14/20; Aqṭāy an inspiring leader and worldly; Yaḥyā malevolent; the heir Muḥammad an extortioner. The Ottoman sultan Aḥmad is the son of the Muḥammad of 1370 and brother of the Emperor's wife ʿĀʾisha, and Yaḥyā of Fās is Jadīla bint Yūsuf's grandson. Both links are in the save.

Invented colour, across this survey and the entries of August 1420: the Emperor finding the old survey and his leaf of four lines; Tīmūr's pyramids of skulls and unbeaten record (history's, not the save's); the envoys' opinions of Aḥmad and Yaḥyā; the old houses' objections to the universities and the Emperor's reply; the secretaries' laughter over Ibn Gharsiya's letter and the margin note (the letter, its argument and "a man is what he knows" are the game's event); the slaves and eunuchs of the household in the Autocracy speech (the game's reform says only that ministers become members of the royal household); the letters to the ṭāʾifas after Fermoselle; Angra's church and the Emperor's remark on it; the instructions to the Cape Verde settlers (the save's colony goal is to form a colonial nation); and the sailors' saying at Daniel's death (the fever and Sanlúcar are in the save). The treasury fell from 1,923 to 277 between the saves of 1416 and 1420, with 565 owed. The save doesn't record what it was spent on.
