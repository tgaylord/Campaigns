With the gold of the peace our lord [Yūsuf](person:yusuf-i) did what the princes of his house have always done when God gave them a season of fortune. He built. In Rabīʿ al-Awwal of the year 750 the masons laid the foundations of a new wing of al-Ḥamrāʾ on the Sabīka hill above [Gharnāṭa](place:gharnata). Its vaults were to be hung with *muqarnas* like the cells of a honeycomb, and its walls cut with verses in praise of the house of Naṣr. The sultan meant it as a letter to Castile and to Fās alike. The house of Naṣr is not a guest in al-Andalus that may be asked to leave. It means to stay, and it builds as those who mean to stay build.

The realm needed telling, for its sovereignty had fallen to two parts in five and the scales of the Strait leaned toward Morocco. The estates were read that month after the peace. The crown held a little more than a quarter of the realm's power, and the Nobility, once nearly half, held less than a third. The Dhimmī, swollen by the Christians of the new lands, held a fifth. In those lands Castilian patriots were gathering in the hills of [Jayyān](place:jayyan) and [Ishbīliyya](place:ishbiliyya). The two princes of the cabinet were sent to bind those cities to the realm, while the plague went on taking their people.

So the sultan sought a weight to set on his own side of the scales. He sent an envoy across the whole length of the sea to al-Qāhira, to the Mamlūk sultan Jamāl al-Dīn, keeper of the two holy cities and lord of Egypt and Syria. The embassy cost a hundred and seven dīnārs, a quarter of a year's income. The envoy carried a plea: the last Muslim kingdom of the West stood alone between the Christian crowns and a Marīnid ally who wished to be its master.

The answer came on the fourteenth of Rabīʿ al-Awwal, and it was more than the court had dared hope. The Mamlūk wrote as a brother in faith. He granted an alliance with Gharnāṭa, and sent Mamlūk horsemen and foot to stand in the sultan's army, the slave-soldiers who had broken the Mongols at ʿAyn Jālūt. He promised gold and men every year besides. Within three days the realm's sovereignty rose above the half again, for the first time since the summer of 747.

But friendship has its price. The court's envoys were stretched across too many courts: Fās, Tūnis and now al-Qāhira, with too few clerks and too little silver to keep them all. The strain fell on the crown itself. The sultan let the alliance with Tūnis lapse quietly, and kept the Moroccan one for now, to see what the Marīnids would do.

*The believers are but brothers, so make settlement between your brothers.*
