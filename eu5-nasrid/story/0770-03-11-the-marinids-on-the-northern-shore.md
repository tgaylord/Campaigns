While Gharnāṭa was winning its freedom, Fās was winning something else. On the thirteenth of Dhū al-Ḥijja 766 the Marīnids made their own peace with Qashtāla and took from it the whole northern shore of the Strait. That was eleven towns, from [Runda](place:runda) in its mountains and [Marballa](place:marballa) on the sea to [Qādis](place:qadis), [Sharīsh](place:sharish), [Jabal Ṭāriq](place:jabal-tariq), [Ṭarīf](place:tarifa) and [al-Jazīra al-Khaḍrāʾ](place:al-jazira-al-khadra). These were the very gates through which Ṭāriq had come into al-Andalus. At court men said it was better that the shore should be held by Muslims than by Christians. The wiser ones answered that it was better still that it should not be held by Fās.

Fās soon showed what it intended. In Rabīʿ al-Thānī 767 the Marīnid sultan, [Abū ʿInān Fāris](person:abu-inan-faris), made nine of the eleven towns into a fief under a lord of his own. He gave it the name the rebels had used thirteen years before, the Kingdom of Sevilla. [Ishbīliyya](place:ishbiliyya) itself was Gharnāṭa's and had been since 749, so the kingdom was named for a city it did not hold, and anyone could see which city it meant to claim.

Peace lasted two years, and they were not quiet ones. Qashtāla, beaten, began to court Gharnāṭa with sweet words. In Ṣafar 768 the alliance with Cairo lapsed. Saʿd's wife [Aiman](person:aiman-mamluk) wrote to her father, and in Jumādā al-Ūlā the sultan renewed it. Then Fās took up its old demand again. In Dhū al-Ḥijja 768 the dīwān learned that the Marīnid sultan claimed the same right he had used in 761, to demand Gharnāṭa's submission as his subject.

The year 769 was worse. The Marīnids broke the trade agreements. Their envoys spoke of the claims of the Kingdom of Sevilla as if it were a real crown and not a garrison. On the twenty-first of Ramaḍān they insulted the sultan's ambassadors before the whole court of Fās. That gave Gharnāṭa a just cause of its own, and the sultan put it away for later. In the month of the pilgrimage Qashtāla closed its markets to Gharnāṭa's merchants. In Ṣafar 770 it declared itself guarantor of the independence of al-Gharb, Gharnāṭa's newest ṭāʾifa, as though [Rodrigo of Silves](person:rodrigo-of-silves) needed a Christian king to protect him from his own lord. The next day the sultan's agents were in Ukshūnuba buying up the loyalty of al-Gharb's notables. Other agents had been in Fās since Dhū al-Ḥijja.

On the eleventh of Rabīʿ al-Awwal 770 Fās declared war, as everyone had known it would. Tilimsān, Tāfīlālt, Tuwāt and the puppet kingdom marched with it. Gharnāṭa had its three ṭāʾifas. It also had something it had never had before: the sultan of Egypt, and with him every vassal of the Mamlūk house from Makka and Madīna to Barqa and the Western Oases. Saʿd heard the declaration read and is said to have answered: *Let them come, and let them remember Sharīsh.*

*Permission to fight has been given to those who are fought against, because they were wronged. And indeed, God is competent to give them victory.*
