The Emperor [Muḥammad](person:muhammad-ibn-ismail-ibn-saad) had been twenty-five, and judged unfit to rule by everyone who knew him, when he was given the fleet. He had never forgotten what it had done for him. The court had called him slow; the captains had called him admiral. He had learned the names of harbour-masters, the price of pitch and the turn of the winds off Cape Saint Vincent, and he had come back from the sea a man who could win a battle at the mouth of the Duero. In the winter of 841 he did for his grandson what had been done for him.

[Yūsuf](person:yusuf-ibn-ismail-ibn-muhammad) was the eldest son of the heir [Ismāʿīl](person:ismail-ibn-muhammad-ibn-ismail), by Tella of the house of Fās, and second in line to the throne after his father. He was sixteen. He had been born at [Gharnāṭa](place:gharnata) and given the most expensive schooling the realm could buy, and his tutors had called him shrewd before he could ride. He was not slow, and he had not been judged unfit by anyone. The Emperor made him admiral of the fleet anyway, because, he said, a prince who had never been to sea did not know what his empire was made of.

Yūsuf had already married three wives, and the court read them as a map of the realm his grandfather meant him to rule. The first was [Amie](person:amie-of-mali), a daughter of Mansa [Tiramakhan](person:tiramakhan-mali) of Mālī, who had come north by the sea road to Ishbīliya with a dowry the treasurers spoke of for a month. The second was [Fāṭima](person:fatima-bint-yusuf-ibn-ismail), daughter of the late [Yūsuf ibn Ismāʿīl](person:yusuf-ibn-ismail), lord of [Banbalūna](place:banbaluna), so that the heir's heir was bound by marriage to the ṭāʾifas of the north at the moment the Emperor was demanding more of them. The third was [Muʾmina](person:mumina-bint-muslih-al-din), daughter of Muṣliḥ al-Dīn, a Naṣrid of the court, so that Ishbīliya would not feel left out. The gold country, the frontier and the palace now shared a household.

The realm the young admiral would sail for had changed again. [Barshalūna](place:barshaluna) and all of Catalonia had come into the crown, and the Emperor was building protected harbours at Barcelona, Girona, Tarragona, Tortosa and Perpignan. For the first time in the memory of the court, the temper of al-Andalus leaned more to the sea than to the land. In Burghush the boy [Khālid](person:khalid-ibn-abi-hammu) had come of age and taken back his ṭāʾifa from the Emperor's regency. And on 7 Jumādā al-Ūlā 841 the Emperor's mother [Bernarda](person:bernarda-of-murcia) died at seventy. She had been born an heiress of Mursiyya, Castilian in speech, and raised at the court of Qashtāla, she had been the wife of one Emperor and the mother of another, and she had lived to see Qashtāla vanish from the map.

*It is He who has made you successors upon the earth and has raised some of you above others in degrees, that He may try you through what He has given you.*
