In the spring Fās began to pour men across the Strait, and it did not stop. Its fleet was eighty ships strong and ruled the water, so it could land wherever it chose, and it chose everywhere. On the twenty-sixth of Shaʿbān 770 a Granadan column of two thousand one hundred attacked a Marīnid force of equal size at [Sharīsh](place:sharish) and was destroyed. [Murād](person:murad) retook the town in Ramaḍān with seven thousand men and five hundred of al-Gharb, and in Shawwāl he cleared [Shalūqa](place:shaluqa) at the mouth of the river.

The Marīnid general that year was [ʿUthmān](person:osman-marinid). He was a level-headed man, born in the saddle, and he understood what the Granadans did not: [Qādis](place:qadis) mattered more than anything else on the coast. In Shawwāl he landed beside it, wiped out the five hundred and eighty Granadans who held the siege lines, and brought the fortress fresh men and grain. On the twentieth of Dhū al-Qaʿda [Khālid ibn Yaḥyā](person:khalid-ibn-yahya) came to take it back. Khālid was the architect who had sat in the dīwān since he was sixteen and had now been made a general. He brought six thousand four hundred and sixty men, Granadans, Gharbīs and the men of Mursiyya, against four thousand seven hundred and seventy behind the causeway and the walls. It was the worst day of the war. Three thousand eight hundred and sixty of ours were left on the causeway, in the shallows and before the gate, and fourteen hundred and twenty of theirs. Khālid brought the rest home, and no one at court reproached him for it. Everyone knew who had ordered the assault.

The treasury could not replace those men, so the sultan borrowed from his own estates. Seven loans of sixty-seven dīnārs each, four hundred and sixty-nine in all, bought a company of four thousand eight hundred mercenaries. They were half of Khālid's army by autumn. The rest of the money came wherever the sultan could squeeze it: from the estates, from the ṭāʾifas, from what Fās had conceded, and from Egypt.

In Ṣafar 771 a Marīnid army of four thousand eight hundred landed at [Mālaqa](place:malaqa). It caught the host of Mursiyya, four hundred and ten men under [Juan Manuel](person:juan-manuel-enriquez), and destroyed it. Then ʿUthmān marched inland with eight thousand men to cut the realm in two at [Antaqīra](place:antaqira), on the road between Mālaqa and Gharnāṭa.

Khālid met him there on the thirteenth of Rabīʿ al-Awwal with six thousand two hundred. Those who fought that day said the line nearly broke in the afternoon. The mercenaries on the left gave ground and the Marīnid horse came through the gap. Khālid filled it himself with the last of the sultan's guard. The line held. When ʿUthmān drew off at dusk, four thousand and ninety of his men lay before it, against nine hundred and sixty of ours. No battle of the war killed more, and none mattered more. Antaqīra was the high-water mark of the Marīnid flood. The heart of the realm was not reached.

Three weeks later Khālid caught eighteen hundred and seventy Marīnids besieging [Ḥiṣn al-Munakkab](place:hisn-al-munakkab) on the coast and broke them, eleven hundred dead for two hundred of ours. In the same days the first Mamlūk army came ashore at [Jarghal](place:jarghal) in the hills above al-Mariyya, five thousand seven hundred soldiers of Egypt on the soil of al-Andalus. But the year belonged to Fās. Of all the north shore, only [Labla](place:labla) still answered to Gharnāṭa. Marīnid armies stood at [Runda](place:runda), [Abdara](place:abdara) and the gates of Mālaqa. The treasury was spending a hundred and seventy-six dīnārs a month and taking in seventy-six.

*O you who have believed, when you encounter a company, stand firm and remember God much, that you may be successful.*
