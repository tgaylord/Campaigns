This note covers three entries: the palace and the harbours, the cape and Fernando Po, and the closed ports.

**What the saves show.** There are three saves (29 August 1442, 6 February 1444, 27 August 1444) and an autosave of 1 February 1447.
- *The estates:* back near the levels the tax rate holds them at by February 1444. The umarāʾ are at 35 and the burghers at 56; by 1447 the umarāʾ are at 40, the burghers at 54 and the rest at 50. Legitimacy is full from 1444.
- *The house:* Prince Yūsuf's four children, their mothers and their birthplaces (Saʿd and Fārūq at Niani) all come from the 1447 autosave, as does the death of Saʿd ibn Ismāʿīl on 3 September 1443.
- *The palace:* a grand palace stands at Seville in the 1447 autosave; Trent's account gives the commission of 10 October 1444.
- *Harbor Administration:* the effects (a naval governor, port proximity −20%, coastal growth +10%, 649 days) are from Trent's screenshot of 30 January 1445. The autosave lists it among the reforms in force, with Autocracy gone. That one was given up to make room for the other is my reading of the reform slots, not something the game states.
- *The sea:* the land-versus-naval value moves from +27 (August 1442) to +86 (February 1447).
- *The treasury and the peace:* 2,484 and 18.7 in August 1444; 570 and 8.3 in February 1447. The saves don't say why. The closed-ports entry sets the fall beside the trade war without claiming it as the cause.
- *The embargoes:* Ireland on 27 May 1444, Aquitaine on 23 November 1444, Flanders on 7 January 1447. Al-Andalus's own closing of its markets to the Angevins on 8 January 1447 comes from the autosave; Trent didn't mention it. The save's dates were used where they differ from Trent's by a few days.

**From Trent's account.** The explorer's cape in mid-March 1446, the merchants researching the Kongo, and Fernando Po withdrawn. The explorer himself (birth at Alcobaça, his own house, his traits and his appointment on 30 October 1445) is from the autosave.

**Invented colour.**
- *Palace entry:* the princes going home, Saʿd's quiet life, the court's joke about heirs on both shores of the desert, the brothers watched as boys (the 1398 succession note supports it), the palace's purpose, the quays, the harbour masters and tax-farmers, and the clerks' talk.
- *Cape entry:* ʿAbd al-Jalīl's character beyond his traits, the officers' saying, the pilots' fifty years down the coast, the month of the pilgrimage as the season, the open water and the sea of the Indies, the Kongo's goods, and the lords' and burghers' reactions.
- *Closed-ports entry:* the Irish king as the Angevins' man, the Gascon wine trade, the Andalusī houses at Bruges, and the young and old merchants.
