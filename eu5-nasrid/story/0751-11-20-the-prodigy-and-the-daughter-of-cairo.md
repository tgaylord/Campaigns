In Dhū al-Qaʿda of the year 751 a letter came from al-Qāhira, sealed with the seal of the Mamlūk sultan. Jamāl al-Dīn agreed to give his daughter [Aiman](person:aiman-mamluk) in marriage to [Saʿd](person:saad-ibn-yusuf), son of our lord [Yūsuf](person:yusuf-i) and heir to the throne of Gharnāṭa, when the two came of age. So the alliance made in the embassy of 750 became a bond of blood between the house of Naṣr and the lords of Egypt and Syria. The betrothal sealed with the Marīnids in the year of Saʿd's birth was not forgotten, but the court no longer spoke of it.

In that same month the chancellor brought the sultan a report on his son, who was not yet three years old. The tutors of the nursery said the child learned as other children breathe. He knew his letters before he could run, and he asked questions about the stars and the Qurʾān that his teachers could not always answer. The chancellor wrote that his future was bright, and that he was predestined to leave his name in the books of history. The court began to whisper what no one dared say aloud in the plague years: that this child might be the prince to lead the Emirate out of the darkness.

The sultan answered as a father and as a king. He sent to every corner of the Islamic world for the finest teachers that silver could bring to Gharnāṭa. There were grammarians and jurists of the Mālikī school, astronomers and physicians, masters of the Qurʾān and of poetry, from Fās and Tilimsān, from Tūnis and al-Qāhira and Dimashq. And because the realm now held nearly as many Christians as Muslims, he sent too for several learned Christians. They were to teach the heir the tongues and laws and customs of the people he would one day rule. Some of the *ʿulamāʾ* frowned at it. The sultan said that a prince who would rule Castilians must first understand them.

The plague had taken the scholars of Gharnāṭa by the half. Now the scholars of the world came to Gharnāṭa for the sake of one small boy. The education of Saʿd would be long and thorough, and the court watched it as a farmer watches the first green shoot after a hard winter.

*He gives wisdom to whom He wills, and whoever has been given wisdom has certainly been given much good.*
