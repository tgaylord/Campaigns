The kings of England and France had been at war, on and off, since before the house of Naṣr had a sultan old enough to remember it. The scribes of the first survey had written in 772 that the two hated each other, and that the French king was losing his kingdom slowly. In Rajab 830 the English went to war again, with all their vassals in France behind them and King Fernando of Qashtāla beside them, and this time the losing was not slow. Fernando made his own peace with the French in the spring and went home. The English did not.

On 19 Ṣafar 832 France made peace. It gave up eighty towns. Picardy and Poitou, from Amiens and Saint-Quentin to Poitiers, went to England itself. The Agenais went to the English duke of Aquitaine, and the country of Langres to the duke of Burgundy, who was England's vassal. The whole of Languedoc, forty-two towns from Toulouse to Carcassonne, Montpellier, Nîmes and Foix, became a new dominion of the English crown, ruled from Narbonne. The ʿulamāʾ of [Ishbīliya](place:ishbiliyya) knew both names. Narbonne was Arbūna, which the armies of the first governors of al-Andalus had taken and held for forty years. Poitiers was the field the Arabs called the Pavement of the Martyrs, where the advance of Islam into the land of the Franks had stopped seven hundred years before. Now both belonged to the king of England.

Three days later the French crown went with them. On 22 Ṣafar 832 Edward of England, twenty-eight years old and five years a king, was made king of France as well. England, France and the Dauphiné had one ruler. [Philippe](person:philippe-vii-of-france), the French king who had lost the war, was allowed to keep a seat in his conqueror's French council, and the court of Ishbīliya thought that the most humiliating part of the peace.

The dīwān heard the news in silence. The Emperor [Ismāʿīl](person:ismail-ibn-saad) had spent six months sending spies into the north to prepare the last war of the Istirdād, and he had counted on Qashtāla being alone. Qashtāla was four towns on the Cantabrian coast with no army, but it had one ally, and that ally was [Edward](person:edward-v-of-england). England was now the second power of the world and stronger with every treaty. It was the only great power that named al-Andalus among its enemies, and its lands now ran along the Pyrenees beside the Ṭāʾifa of Catalonia, within a few days' march of [Barshalūna](place:barshaluna). The great road to [Balansiyya](place:balansiyya) had been built to carry an army to the French border should the Pope ever preach another crusade. The border at its end was now English.

The secretaries of the Dīwān al-Inshāʾ pointed out that England and France would need years to digest each other, that the French nobles had not accepted an English king out of love, and that every English soldier in Languedoc was a soldier who would be needed there. The Emperor listened. Then he ordered his envoys to London and Paris, which now meant the same thing, with letters of congratulation. He also asked the treasurers how many more armouries the empire could afford.

*The Romans have been defeated, in the nearest land. But they, after their defeat, will overcome, within three to nine years.*
