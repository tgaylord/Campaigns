In the month of Rabīʿ al-Thānī in the year 747 the merchants who come by sea from Alexandria and Tūnis brought a tale that the court at first took for a sailors' fable. At Yāyiq, they said, far beyond the Sea of the Khazars in the lands of the Golden Horde, a sickness had risen that no physician could name. It came with swellings under the arm and in the groin and with fever and black blood. A man was well at the dawn prayer and dead by the evening one, and whole towns were given over to the dogs. The sickness moved along the roads of the caravans and the lanes of the ships, westward, as a fire moves along a dry slope.

The sultan did not dismiss the tale. He remembered that a plague goes where trade goes, and that no realm in al-Andalus lives by trade as Gharnāṭa does. The Christians of Qashtāla live in villages and walled farms; our people live crowded in the great cities, in the alleys of the Albayzín, in the markets of [Mālaqa](place:malaqa), in the harbour quarter of [al-Mariyya](place:al-mariyya), where every ship from the east puts in. If the sickness came, it would find us first and find us thick on the ground.

So at the beginning of Rajab, in the same days that Morocco called the realm to war, our lord [Yūsuf](person:yusuf-i) ordered houses of healing (*bīmāristānāt*) raised in the three great cities, [Gharnāṭa](place:gharnata), Mālaqa and al-Mariyya. They were to have wards for the sick, cisterns of running water, and physicians paid from the treasury, Muslim and Jewish alike. The debt had been all but paid that summer and the treasury could bear the cost. The nobles grumbled that silver spent on a rumour was silver not spent on lances. The sultan answered that a lance will not turn a fever.

Those who mocked the order would remember it within two years. Those who praised it would learn that walls and wards slow a pestilence but do not stop it, for God's decree outruns the builder.

*No disaster strikes upon the earth or among yourselves except that it is in a register before We bring it into being. Indeed that, for God, is easy.*
