The Emperor [Ismāʿīl](person:ismail-ibn-saad) had told the dīwān that Catalonia must come into the crown within nine years, and he had not lived to see it. His son did it within eight. On 16 Dhū al-Qaʿda 839 (1 June 1436) the Ṭāʾifa of Catalonia was annexed, a year before the court had expected, and [Barshalūna](place:barshaluna), the great port on the English border, was ruled from [Ishbīliya](place:ishbiliyya) for the first time. Its lord [Francesc](person:francesc-de-montsoriu), son of the Berenguer Ramon who had died at sea, was a Muslim and a grandson of the house of Naṣr through his grandmother. He gave up his throne without a war. The Catalans had been accepted at court for thirteen years, and the merchants of Barshalūna had been trading under the Emperor's peace for sixty. Most of them found that very little changed except the address of the tax-collector.

At the turn of the year 840 the scholars of the court took stock, as they did at the end of every age. They agreed that the age of the rebirth of learning, which the Franks had called the Renaissance, was over, and that a new age had begun, the age of the discovery of the world. The ships of al-Andalus were already in it. They also agreed, more quietly, on something that would not have been believed in [Qurṭuba](place:qurtuba) a hundred years before. The learning, the poetry, the music, the buildings and the manners of al-Andalus were once more what the rest of the world imitated. The books copied in the madrasas of Ishbīliya were read in Cairo and Fās and, in translation, in Paris and Bologna. The courts of Christendom built their gardens with Andalusī water and hired Andalusī masons. In the chanceries of the world al-Andalus was named the first of all realms in culture.

Two months later the new age found its name in Ishbīliya itself. No one had yet found land beyond the Ocean. But the city was full of men who meant to: pilots home from the Azores and the Green Cape arguing over charts in the funduqs by the river, merchants buying shares in voyages that had not yet sailed, scholars of the madrasas asking what the ancients had meant by the Isles of the Blessed, and the crown paying for all of it. By Shaʿbān 840 the scholars of other realms had begun to call it a new way of thinking about the world, and to say that it had been born on the banks of the Wādī al-Kabīr. Perhaps the captains would find something out there.

The old men of the court said that it was the age of the caliphs come again. The Emperor [Muḥammad](person:muhammad-ibn-ismail-ibn-saad), who had never read a book for pleasure in his life, accepted the compliment on behalf of his grandfather, his father and the madrasas they had built in every town, and went back to his ships.

*And thus We have made you a middle nation, that you will be witnesses over the people, and the Messenger will be a witness over you.*
