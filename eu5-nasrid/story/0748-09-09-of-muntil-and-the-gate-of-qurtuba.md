In the autumn the Marīnids tried their fortune in the north. A Moroccan army of four thousand, with a handful of Tunisians, struck into the high plain of La Mancha. On the twenty-seventh of Jumādā al-Ākhira, at [Munṭīl](place:muntil), it met eight thousand Castilians. The Moroccans were beaten and fled. Yet they sold the field dearly: twelve hundred of them fell, and three thousand eight hundred of the enemy.

When the sultan heard of it he marched north to Munṭīl. On the twenty-fourth of Rajab, on the same field where the Moroccans had broken, he met what remained of the Castilian army, some four and a half thousand men, with sixteen thousand of his own. The Christians had not had time to bury their dead from the first battle before they joined them. Three thousand one hundred fell, and we lost five hundred. The jinetes pursued the rest into the hills, and the Moroccans' debt was paid.

Then the host came down again to [Qurṭuba](place:qurtuba), the old seat of the caliphs. The Christians have held it for a hundred and eleven years and made its great mosque into a church. On the ninth of Ramaḍān, the month of Badr, Qashtāla's southern army was rebuilt once more and sent to keep the sultan from the city. It had four thousand four hundred men. The sultan had fifteen thousand seven hundred. The Castilians did not break at once, but when they broke the jinetes rode them down. Three thousand three hundred and fifty Christians died before the gate of Qurṭuba, and two hundred and fifty of ours.

That made five great victories in a year, at Jayyān, al-Quṣayr, Balma, Munṭīl and Qurṭuba. Nearly twenty-four thousand Castilians had fallen in them, and the tyrant's armies in the south had been broken again and again and made whole again from fresh levies. The old soldiers said they could not be made whole for ever. Qashtāla had men still. What it was running out of was men who believed they could win.

*How many a small company has overcome a large company by permission of God.* So it was said of Badr, when the believers were few. In Ramaḍān of 748 the believers were many and the infidels few, and the verse was turned around, and still it held, for God is with the patient.
