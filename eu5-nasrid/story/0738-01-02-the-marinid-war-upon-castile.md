In the name of God, the Merciful, the Compassionate. Here begins the account of the reign of our lord the sultan Abū al-Ḥajjāj [Yūsuf](person:yusuf-i), son of [Ismāʿīl](person:ismail-i), may God make his days a garden and his banners a shade. He sat in the Alhambra above [Gharnāṭa](place:gharnata) in the nineteenth year of his age, and the realm he held was eighteen towns and their districts, close to four hundred and fifty thousand souls. It was a jewel set in a narrow ring, with Qashtāla pressing on the north and the sea at its back.

On the first night of Ramaḍān there passed from this world the lady [Fāṭima bint al-Aḥmar](person:fatima-bint-al-ahmar), daughter of the sultan Muḥammad al-Faqīh, mother of Ismāʿīl and grandmother of our lord. She had seen seventy-six years and seven reigns, and she went out with the crescent of the fast. *Every soul shall taste death*, but few are given so fitting an hour to taste it.

In that same month there came a call from [Fās](place:fas). The Marīnid sultan Abū al-Ḥasan ʿAlī, lord of the Maghrib al-Aqṣā, had made war two years since upon the Banū ʿAbd al-Wād of [Tilimsān](place:tilimsan), and now he called on his ally across the water. On the twentieth of Ramaḍān our lord gave his answer and his word, and three days later [the war](war:fate-of-tlemcen) was ended. Tilimsān bowed its head and became a fief of the Marīnids. Not one Andalusī fell, for the matter was settled before our men had left their homes. Yet the court remembered that the word had been given, and so did Fās.

With the end of Ramaḍān the old contest over the Strait took on a new and sharper form, the one the clerks of the dīwān name its *battle*. On the first day of the contest the tribute owed to Qashtāla was fixed anew as *parias*, gold weighed out for the infidel king Alfonso. On the morning of ʿĪd al-Fiṭr itself, as the city broke its fast, a royal marriage joined our house to the Ḥafṣids of Tūnis, so that the realm might stand upon more than one shore.

The dīwān was filled. Our lord set his elder brother [Faraj ibn Ismāʿīl](person:faraj-ibn-ismail), now named heir, to strengthen the government. On the Day of ʿArafa he charged his cousin [Yūsuf ibn Muḥammad](person:yusuf-ibn-muhammad), a man of seventy in diplomacy and of plain gifts in all else, with the defence of Granadan sovereignty.

Then, on the second day of Muḥarram in the year 738, with the new year barely begun, Abū al-Ḥasan declared [war](war:moroccan-castilian-war) upon Qashtāla to reclaim al-Andalus, and Tilimsān marched under his banner. The alliance gives Abū al-Ḥasan the right to summon our lord, and he has not yet done so. Our lord is bound by tribute to the one side and by his word to the other. As this is written, the sovereignty of Gharnāṭa stands a hair above the half, by the reckoning of the scribes, and the scales of influence lean a quarter of the way toward Qashtāla. Between the lion of Fās and the castle of Qashtāla lies a strait one may cross in an afternoon, and Gharnāṭa stands upon its shore and waits. *And God is the best of planners.*
