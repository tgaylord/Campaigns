While the sultan was in the Upper March, the court at [Gharnāṭa](place:gharnata) was run by a woman, and nobody who knew her was surprised. [Duʿā](person:dua-marinid) the Marīnid had sat in the dīwān for nineteen years, under three husbands and two reigns. She had brought the court foreign learning and the nobles' displeasure with it, and she had spent the last thirteen of those years making the Christians of the new lands into Andalusīs. When our lord [Saʿd](person:saad-ibn-yusuf) rode north, he left the seal with her. The sultan's mother, [Sayyida](person:sayyida-of-fezzan), sat beside her in the dīwān. The court said the realm had never been in more experienced hands.

The ʿulamāʾ had not forgotten the moneylenders. Three years earlier the sultan had told them they were overreacting, and they had nursed the wound since. Duʿā meant to heal it before the army came home. In the spring of 775 a letter came from the camp before [Barshalūna](place:barshaluna), written in the sultan's own hand. It gave her leave to summon the Jamāʿa. The question she put to it was the tithe: who should collect the dues of the faithful and the taxes of the dhimmīs, and by what rule. Across the realm, and above all in the new lands, collection had grown irregular. Each town paid what its governor could squeeze out of it, and the Christians and Jews of the valley never knew from one year to the next what they owed. Duʿā proposed to give the whole business to the ʿulamāʾ, under one rule for the realm. They would gain the revenue and the authority over the dhimmī communities. The crown would gain a tax that was reliably paid.

The ʿulamāʾ were delighted. The nobles were not. Every dirham the scholars collected was a dirham the lords of the valley could not, and they made their price plain. Duʿā paid it. It was a handful of small privileges, nothing the sultan would notice from Catalonia, and each of them bound the peasants of the nobles' estates a little more tightly to the land. On 19 Dhū al-Qaʿda 775 the Jamāʿa voted the measure through. For ten years the tithe would be regulated across the realm, the ʿulamāʾ would be content, and the treasury would collect a twentieth more of what it was owed.

Duʿā wrote to the sultan that the scholars were reconciled to him and the dhimmīs would know what they owed. She also wrote that the nobles had been bought cheaply. The sultan wrote back that nothing the nobles sold was cheap, and that the peasants would remember the price after the lords had forgotten it. But he did not undo the bargain. He had a war to finish.

*And consult them in the matter; then when you have decided, rely upon Allāh.*
