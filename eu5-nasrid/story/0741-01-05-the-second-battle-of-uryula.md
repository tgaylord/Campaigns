All that winter the host lay before [Uryūla](place:uryula). When the supplies ran short, the Volunteers of the Faith under [Yaḥyā Raḥḥū](person:yahya-rahhu) drew back to find bread, while Ibn al-Khaṭīb kept the siege lines with the rest. The sultan raised more men to carry food and paid for everything with borrowed gold. By the ninth of Ramaḍān in the year 740 the treasury owed the estates of the realm more than thirteen hundred and fifty dīnārs. The estates lent freely and asked their price in return. With every dīnār lent, their voice in the dīwān grew louder and the crown's grew fainter. By spring the nobility held more than half the power of the state and the crown about a seventh.

On the second of Dhū al-Ḥijja, after nearly seven months, Uryūla opened its gates. That same week came the news that undid the joy of it. Four days earlier the Castilians had fallen on the Tlemcenī garrison of [Mursiyya](place:mursiyya) and killed seven hundred of them, and now they besieged the town. The road by which the host had come was closed. There was no way home but through the enemy, so the host stood where it was and made ready. And in the realm the nobles, their satisfaction worn thin, began to mutter in their castles.

They did not have to wait long. On the twenty-third of Dhū al-Ḥijja the kings of Nabarra, Mayūrqa and Arghūn came down together to throw the Muslims into the sea, thirteen thousand of them under the same captain who had been beaten before the same walls. Eighteen thousand of ours held the ground and the town behind them. The fight lasted into the new year. On the fifth of Muḥarram 741 the Christians broke, leaving seven and a half thousand of their number on the field, more than half their army. We lost two thousand three hundred. Twice now [Ibn al-Khaṭīb](person:ibn-al-khatib) had beaten that captain before Uryūla, and the war turned another quarter in our favour.

*How many a small company has overcome a great one, by the leave of God.*
