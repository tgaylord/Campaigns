*And the heaven He raised and imposed the balance, that you not transgress within the balance.* So God has set a balance over all things, and over the Strait of the Pillars more than most. On one pan of the scales sit the three crowns of the Christians and on the other the Marīnids of Fās, and between them Gharnāṭa, the smallest weight, which both would gladly throw onto their own side. In the summer of 747 the scales began to tip. The realm's own sovereignty, the measure of how far it was master of itself, slipped below the half for the first time in the reign. The dīwān marked it, and did not yet know what it foretold.

On the twenty-eighth of Jumādā al-Ākhira the Marīnid sultan declared war on Qashtāla to reclaim al-Andalus, six years after his host had been broken. The tyrant of Qashtāla called on his friends, and Portugal came in, and Arghūn with Majorca and Cagliari. For the first time all three crowns of Iberia stood in one line. With Morocco marched Tāfīlālt, Tuwāt and Tilimsān, and three days later Tūnis and its marches. Then the Marīnid's letter came to Gharnāṭa: our brother in faith expected us to take the field.

The council was divided. We were the only Muslim power on this side of the water and the only one that would face all three crowns at once. We were Qashtāla's tributary and paid its *parias*. The debt to the estates had only just been paid. The Catalan traders, driven off by the war with Arghūn, had only just begun to come back to the markets, and a new war with Arghūn would drive them off again. And the old men remembered that Morocco had dragged the realm into its quarrels before and then left it to bear the cost.

But the sultan weighed it and judged that the scales would not wait on Gharnāṭa's caution. On the first of Rajab he answered the summons. The Ṭāʾifa of [Balansiyya](place:balansiyya) marched with us, and the land there now held more Muslims than Christians, ninety-two thousand to ninety. Taking up arms against our overlord suspended the tribute, and the war itself would decide the realm's independence. The reparations Arghūn had paid since the peace of 741 ceased the same day.

Then the Volunteers of the Faith rose again. These were the same Ghuzāt who had broken Arghūn at Uryūla, and their old commander [Yaḥyā Raḥḥū](person:yahya-rahhu) led them once more: twenty-four companies of men who asked no wage but the war against the united crowns. By the twenty-second of Rajab the host was raised. There were seventeen regiments of the *mutaṭawwiʿa*, the feudal levies of the Nobility, the knights and the jinetes, and a second company of hired soldiers taken on two days before. The treasury held fifty-five dīnārs. The war was costing forty a month against an income of twenty-seven. No one in the dīwān pretended that the realm could bear it for long.
