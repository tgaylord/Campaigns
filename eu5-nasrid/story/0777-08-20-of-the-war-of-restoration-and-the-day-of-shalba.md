The truce with Qashtāla ran out on 4 Muḥarram 777, and the Emperor did not let a day of the new year pass. Beyond the Pyrenees the French were pushing the English into the sea. Once they had finished, the Pope at Rūmiya would rally them against al-Andalus, and every advance the Emperor made after that would be blunted by French lances. *We cannot afford to wait*, he told the dīwān. War was declared on Qashtāla that day. The claim was the Reconquest of Iberia, drawn up four years before, but the heralds proclaimed it as the war of the Aragonese restoration. [Saraqusṭa](place:saraqusta) and the heart of old Arghūn, from Jaca to Teruel, lay in Castilian hands, and the renewed kingdom had a right to them.

A levy was laid on the nobles and on the ṭāʾifas, in gold and in men. The treasury, which had held two hundred and eighty-six dīnārs in Shawwāl, held fifteen hundred and seventy-nine by the declaration. Rūmiya and Lisbon answered the Castilian call: al-Burtughāl, whose king was also king of Qashtāla, and the Pope with his vassals of Faenza and Siena. Miṣr answered ours, and the four ṭāʾifas marched beside the Emperor. At the declaration he had three thousand three hundred regulars under arms, and the Castilian king had seventeen hundred and fifty. Nobody had yet called out the levies.

They were called out over the summer and autumn. The campaign opened in the east in Rajab. The ḥājib [Francesc](person:francesc-de-montsoriu) had brought six thousand Catalans and Algarvians to the outskirts of [Balansiyya](place:balansiyya), and on 17 Rajab, at [Šalba](place:shalba) in the hills behind the coast, the Castilian general [Alfonso de Oñate](person:alfonso-de-onate) fell on them with nearly twenty-five thousand. The Emperor was marching to them with ten thousand five hundred, and Valencia's levies with him, but the Catalans did not hold until he came. They lost three thousand two hundred men to Castile's eight hundred and sixty and broke for the coast.

That left the Emperor facing Oñate's whole army on Oñate's chosen ground: hills, forest and a river, everything that hobbles horse and favours the defender. Retreat would give up the country around Balansiyya. On 18 Rajab he gave battle, thirteen thousand eight hundred against twenty-three thousand nine hundred.

It went on for a month, into Shaʿbān. The Emperor did what the Andalusī horse had always done. He threw his cavalry at both Castilian flanks to sweep them away before the weight of the Castilian foot could tell. On the open plain before Barshalūna two years earlier that had won the day. In the broken country of Šalba the horse could not close the jaws fast enough. The centre held, by a thread, through weeks of grinding, while the wings were worn down against twice their numbers. On 20 Shaʿbān the Emperor drew off what was left. Eight thousand one hundred and seventy of his men were dead or scattered, against seven thousand five hundred and twenty of Oñate's. Castile had lost nearly as many as he had, and it could spare them; he could not. The war score fell by twelve. The court said it was the price of fighting on unsuitable ground, and the Emperor did not argue with it.

He levied two thousand more men from the nobles, and he listened to [Duʿā](person:dua-marinid). Since the last war there had been a warm silence between Gharnāṭa and Fās. It was not hostile, but on our side it was distrustful. The Marīnids had courted the Emirate and then stabbed it in the back the moment they could, and the court had not forgotten. Duʿā argued that the time had come to renew the alliance all the same. The expanded bureaucracy could carry the embassy, she said, and it would be months before the first Mamlūk army could reach Iberia. There was a bond now as well. Abū ʿInān's heir, [Abū al-Faḍl](person:abu-al-fadl-marinid), had lately married the Emperor's sister [Jadīla](person:jadila-bint-yusuf). Between Jadīla and Duʿā there was enough to open a dialogue between what had once been good friends.

The envoys rode for Fās, and a pact was offered. On 22 Shaʿbān the alliance was signed. Fās had a truce with Qashtāla that would not end until the autumn of the year after next, so it could not join the war before then. Its envoys swore it would help when it could. The Emperor's council was not sure their word was worth believing.

*If a wound has touched you, a wound like it has touched the others. These are the days We alternate among the people.*
