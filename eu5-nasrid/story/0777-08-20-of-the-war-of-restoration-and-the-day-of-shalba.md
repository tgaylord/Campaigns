The truce with Qashtāla ran out on 4 Muḥarram 777, and the Emperor [Saʿd](person:saad-ibn-yusuf) did not let a day of the new year pass. Beyond the Pyrenees the French were pushing the English into the sea. Once they had finished, the Pope at Avignon would rally them against al-Andalus, and every advance the Emperor made after that would be blunted by French lances. *We cannot afford to wait*, he told the dīwān. War was declared on Qashtāla that day. The claim the dīwān had drawn up four years before reached to all the peninsula the Christians had taken, but the heralds proclaimed the war as the Aragonese restoration. [Saraqusṭa](place:saraqusta) and the heart of old Arghūn, from Jaca to Teruel, lay in Castilian hands, and the renewed kingdom had a right to them.

A levy was laid on the nobles and on the ṭāʾifas, in gold and in men. The treasury, which had held two hundred and eighty-six dīnārs in Shawwāl, held fifteen hundred and seventy-nine by the declaration. Al-Ushbūna and Avignon answered the Castilian call: al-Burtughāl, whose king was also king of Qashtāla, and the Pope with his vassals of Faenza and Siena. Miṣr answered ours, and the four ṭāʾifas marched beside the Emperor. At the declaration he had three thousand three hundred regulars under arms, and the Castilian king had seventeen hundred and fifty. Nobody had yet called out the levies.

They were called out over the summer and autumn. The campaign opened in the east in Rajab. The ḥājib [Francesc](person:francesc-de-montsoriu) had brought six thousand Catalans and Algarvians to the outskirts of [Balansiyya](place:balansiyya), and on 17 Rajab, at [Shalba](place:shalba) in the hills behind the coast, the Castilian general [Alfonso de Oñate](person:alfonso-de-onate) fell on them with nearly twenty-five thousand. The Emperor was marching to them with ten thousand five hundred, and Balansiyya's levies with him, but the Catalans did not hold until he came. They lost three thousand two hundred men to Qashtāla's eight hundred and sixty and broke for the coast.

That left the Emperor facing Oñate's whole army on Oñate's chosen ground: hills, forest and a river, everything that hobbles horse and favours the defender. Retreat would give up the country around Balansiyya. On 18 Rajab he gave battle, thirteen thousand eight hundred against twenty-three thousand nine hundred.

It went on for a month, into Shaʿbān. The Emperor did what the Andalusī horse had always done. He threw his cavalry at both Castilian flanks to sweep them away before the weight of the Castilian foot could tell. On the open plain before [Barshalūna](place:barshaluna) two years earlier that had won the day. In the broken country of Shalba the horse could not close the jaws fast enough. The centre held, by a thread, through weeks of grinding, while the wings were worn down against twice their numbers. On 20 Shaʿbān the Emperor drew off what was left. He had lost eight thousand one hundred and seventy men, against seven thousand five hundred and twenty of Oñate's. Qashtāla had lost nearly as many as he had, and it could spare them; he could not. The old soldiers said it was the price of fighting on unsuitable ground, and the Emperor did not argue with them.

He levied two thousand more men from the nobles, and he listened to [Duʿāʾ](person:dua-marinid). Since the last war there had been a warm silence between [Gharnāṭa](place:gharnata) and [Fās](place:fas). It was not hostile, but on our side it was distrustful. The Marīnids had courted Gharnāṭa and then stabbed it in the back the moment they could, and the court had a long memory. Duʿāʾ argued that the time had come to renew the alliance all the same. The dīwān's clerks had grown with the realm and could carry the embassy, she said, and it would be months before the first Mamlūk army could reach Iberia. There was a bond now as well. [Abū ʿInān](person:abu-inan-faris)'s heir, [Abū al-Faḍl](person:abu-al-fadl-marinid), had lately married the Emperor's sister [Jadīla](person:jadila-bint-yusuf). Between Jadīla and Duʿāʾ there was enough to open a dialogue between what had once been good friends.

The envoys rode for Fās, and a pact was offered. On 22 Shaʿbān the alliance was signed. Fās had a truce with Qashtāla that would not end until the autumn of the year after next, so it could not join the war before then. Its envoys swore it would help when it could. The Emperor's council was not sure their word was worth believing.

*O you who have believed, if you support Allāh, He will support you and plant firmly your feet.*
