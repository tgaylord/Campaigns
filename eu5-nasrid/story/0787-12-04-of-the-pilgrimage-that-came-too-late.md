The pilgrimage was put off once more. In Rabīʿ al-Awwal 787 the lords of the Aragonese uplands rose against King [Alfons](person:alfons-de-borja) of the renewed Arghūn, and proclaimed a County of Teruel in the inner country of his kingdom. It was a small rising, but it was a rising in a ṭāʾifa the Emperor had made and promised to protect, and he would not leave for Makka with it unsettled. It took the summer. On 16 Rajab 787 the County of Teruel was crushed and its towns returned to King Alfons. That same day the Emperor's caravan left [Ishbīliya](place:ishbiliyya) for the east.

It was as fine as the caravan his father had led thirty-four years before. There were camels and horses and the scholars of the court, gifts for the sharīf of Makka, and alms for the poor of the two holy cities. The crown prince [Ismāʿīl](person:ismail-ibn-saad), nineteen years old, ruled as regent in his father's absence. He ruled well, by the dīwān's account. He had a gift for sending a minister away from an audience sure that the prince agreed with him, whatever the prince meant to do. That gift did not serve him in the harem. His mother [Duʿāʾ](person:dua-marinid) and the Emperor's first wife [Aiman](person:aiman-mamluk) had been rivals since the princes were children, and with the Emperor gone they quarrelled openly. They argued over precedence and households, and over whose son the court should look to. The regent learned that it was easier to govern an empire than to keep peace between two women who had each borne the Emperor a son.

In Ramaḍān the caravan reached al-Qāhira, the city of a thousand minarets, which swallows a caravan the moment it passes the northern gate. Above the noise and spice and roasting meat rose the mosque of al-Azhar, whose scholars were counted among the finest in the Muslim world. The Emperor lingered. He sat with them for a month, and he held long talks with his brother-in-law the sultan [Būlād](person:bolot-mamluk) about the fleet, the Maghrib and the Christians of the north. He said afterwards that it was the best month of his reign. Then he pushed on for Makka.

He came too late. When the caravan reached the holy city, the standing at ʿArafāt had passed: the hours between noon and sunset when every pilgrim of the season stands together on the plain beneath the Mount of Mercy, praying and asking forgiveness. A saying of the Prophet holds that the Ḥajj is nothing more or less than that single afternoon, and no rite performed afterwards can complete the pilgrimage. The Emperor heard the news in silence. Then he gave the order the caravan dreaded: they had tarried too long, and it must all be done again. The caravan broke up and turned for home.

But misfortune turned to fortune on the road back. The scholars the Emperor had sat with in al-Qāhira had taken his measure as he had taken theirs. When he offered them halls, stipends and students in Ishbīliya, many of them came west with him. The Jamāʿa had to be persuaded to pay for them. The ʿulamāʾ backed the measure warmly, since it was their own order the Emperor proposed to enlarge, and the assembly voted to sponsor theological seminaries across the realm for ten years. A pilgrimage that never reached ʿArafāt had brought al-Azhar to Ishbīliya.

*But perhaps you hate a thing and it is good for you; and Allāh knows, while you know not.*
