When the peace with Fās was sealed, our lord [Saʿd](person:saad-ibn-yusuf) ordered a count of the realm, as his grandfather's grandfathers had done after every war: how many souls, of what tongue, of what faith. The scribes worked through Ṣafar, and their reckoning is set down here, because it is the measure of everything this book is about.

In the valley of the great river, the lands won from Qashtāla in 749, seven of every hundred souls had been Andalusī at the Peace of Qurṭuba. On the eve of the Marīnid war it was thirty-three. In faith the change was slower: eight in a hundred had been Muslim in 749, and nineteen were by the eve of the war. Twelve towns of the valley now counted themselves Andalusī, [Qarmūna](place:qarmuna) the latest of them, on the eve of the war. Only one was yet Muslim by the greater part of its people, and that one was [Qurṭuba](place:qurtuba). There the faithful had been twenty-seven in a hundred at the peace and were now fifty-nine. [Ishbīliyya](place:ishbiliyya) had gone from fourteen to thirty-one and [Jayyān](place:jayyan) from none to thirty. Úbeda, at the far end of the valley, had gone from none to ten. It had heard the call last.

The northern shore taught the scribes something they did not like. [Qādis](place:qadis), [Sharīsh](place:sharish), [Shalūqa](place:shaluqa), [Jabal Ṭāriq](place:jabal-tariq) and [al-Jazīra al-Khaḍrāʾ](place:al-jazira-al-khadra) had been Castilian towns when Fās took them five years before. They came back to the house of Naṣr already Muslim by the greater part of their people. In five years the Marīnids had done there what twenty years of libraries and madrasas had not done in the valley. The ʿulamāʾ of Gharnāṭa praised it. The older men of the dīwān asked how it had been done. They reminded the court that there is no compulsion in religion, and that what is made by the sword can be unmade by the sword.

Across the whole realm the count was this. Of seven hundred and forty-nine thousand souls, three hundred and sixty-eight thousand were Muslims and three hundred and thirty-three thousand Christians, with forty-seven thousand Jews beside them. Four hundred and seven thousand were Andalusīs and two hundred and eighty-nine thousand Castilians. The Muslims had been the larger faith before, by a few thousand, and lost that place when the Portuguese south came in. Now they held it by thirty-five thousand. The realm of Gharnāṭa was Muslim by the count of its own people, and not only by the faith of its prince.

The call has been made. It is being answered, slowly, in Arabic first and in prayer after, and town by town. The madrasas will go on teaching for a hundred years. This book will go on as long as they do.

*And say: The truth has come, and falsehood has departed. Indeed, falsehood is ever bound to depart.*
