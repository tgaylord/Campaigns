Book I is written in full now. Two causes come from Trent, not the saves. Stability fell from 34 to 7 because of the Jamāʿa's reforms while the debt chipped away at the crown's authority. The burghers soured in 1341 because the crown was squeezing every source of revenue (higher tariffs, small cuts) and the war with Aragon had broken the ties with the Catalan traders, which were only starting to recover in 1346. Why Ibn al-Khaṭīb went over to Castile stays open on purpose. The save only shows him as Frederick, Catholic, a Castilian admiral from 8 July 1341. The court's three rumours are invented, and Trent chose to keep them as the chronicle's answer. Also invented colour: the flowers at Ibn al-Khaṭīb's homecoming, the debate in the Jamāʿa, and the wedding in the Alhambra. The Jamāʿa's change of purpose and the marriage during it are Trent's.
