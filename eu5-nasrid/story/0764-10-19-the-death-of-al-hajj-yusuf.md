On the fifth of Shaʿbān in the year 764, the truce with Qashtāla ran out, and our lord [Yūsuf](person:yusuf-i) did not wait an hour longer. He had a just cause, and he had the ṭāʾifas of Balansiyya and Mursiyya, whole and untouched. He also had a rival who could barely stand. The Marīnids held fifty-eight Castilian towns, as far as [Ṭulayṭula](place:tulaytula) itself, and the tyrant of Qashtāla had fewer than two hundred men left to call up. The treasury held fifty-seven dīnārs. The sultan said that a war for freedom is not fought with what is in the treasury, and he marched north.

His plan was simple. Portugal had made its own peace with Fās and its army was whole, so Portugal would be knocked out first, before it could join the others. The army went west through the hills toward the Guadiana. In Shawwāl came word that Arghūn had joined Qashtāla. A year earlier the two had been at war with each other.

The sultan never reached Portugal. On the nineteenth of Shawwāl, at [Ayamūnt](place:ayamunt) where the Guadiana meets the sea, al-Ḥājj Yūsuf died on the march, ten weeks into the war he had waited thirty years to fight. He was forty-five. The physicians spoke of the heat of the road and a fever that came on in the night. The soldiers said only that he had gone to God with his face turned toward the enemy, and they asked for nothing better for themselves.

Consider what he left. He came to the throne at fifteen, lord of eighteen towns in the mountains, tributary to Qashtāla and in debt to his own estates. He left forty-three towns, the valley of the great river and Qurṭuba of the caliphs, a treasury that had once been free of debt, and a sovereignty the court reckoned complete. He had made the pilgrimage and fed the poor of Makka. He had outlived the pestilence. In his last year he had begun the Qaṣr al-Riyāḍ al-Saʿīd in the heart of the Alhambra, with its lions and its vaults of muqarnas, so that the house of Naṣr would have a palace worthy of what it had become. He did not live to see it finished.

His son [Saʿd](person:saad-ibn-yusuf) was fifteen, the same age his father had been, and the court said there was no child like him in all of Islam. But he could not rule until he came of age in Rabīʿ al-Thānī. The dīwān named a regent, [Naṣr al-Istijjī](person:nasr-al-istijji), a noble of the house of Istijja. He was a man of no great gifts, chosen perhaps because no one feared him. Within days of the burial he had married [Duʿāʾ](person:dua-marinid), the late sultan's Marīnid widow and minister. The court noticed. The army, still in the field, went on without its sultan, and the treasury held twenty-four dīnārs.

*Every soul will taste death, and you will only be given your full reward on the Day of Resurrection.*
