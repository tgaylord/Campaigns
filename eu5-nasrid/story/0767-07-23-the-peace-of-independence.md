In Rabīʿ al-Awwal of the year 766 [Aiman](person:aiman-mamluk) gave the sultan his first child, a daughter. He named her [Umm al-Fatḥ](person:umm-al-fath-bint-saad), mother of victory, which was either a prayer or a boast. By spring the chancellor was recording a new title in the registers of the dīwān. The court had stopped calling Saʿd the prodigy. It called him *al-Fātiḥ*, the Conqueror. He was seventeen.

The war in the east still had to be finished. On the twenty-fourth of Shawwāl, at Cañete, where the Portuguese had broken the men of Balansiyya the year before, sixteen hundred Aragonese attacked Murād's army of five thousand seven hundred. Twelve hundred and sixty of them were left on the field, against a hundred and ninety of ours. Arghūn had joined the war for an ally's sake and gained nothing from it. On the fifteenth of Dhū al-Ḥijja it made its own peace and agreed to pay reparations for ten years.

That left Qashtāla, alone, as Gharnāṭa had been alone so often. On the twenty-second of Rabīʿ al-Thānī 767 Murād found its last army in the field at San Clemente, in La Mancha. There were nine hundred and fifty men, and at their head was a man the court knew well. It was [Ibn al-Khaṭīb](person:ibn-al-khatib), the vizier who had gone over to the Cross a quarter of a century before and served Qashtāla ever since as Frederick, its admiral. Murād had six thousand two hundred and fifty men. Eight hundred and forty Castilians died, a hundred and five were taken, and thirty-one Granadans fell. The renegade escaped, as he always did. The army pushed on into La Mancha and up the road to [Ṭulayṭula](place:tulaytula), the old city of the Goths, and held it when the envoys came.

A month before the peace, Duʿāʾ gave the sultan a son, [Ismāʿīl](person:ismail-ibn-saad), and the realm had an heir of the sultan's own blood. The ʿulamāʾ remembered whose widow his mother was and said nothing.

On the twenty-third of Rajab 767 the peace was sealed, and the tyrant of Qashtāla let Gharnāṭa go. The house of Naṣr, which had paid tribute to the Castilian crown for longer than anyone living could remember, declared itself free and subject to no one but God. Qashtāla gave up the lands of Niebla and [Walba](place:walba): six towns between the Guadalquivir and the Guadiana. Among them was [Ayamūnt](place:ayamunt), where al-Ḥājj Yūsuf had died on the march, so the son brought the place of his father's death into the realm. Qashtāla paid a sum into the treasury and owed reparations for ten years. Ṭulayṭula was given back, burned and half emptied, its people fewer by a third than they had been seven years before.

Eight days later the sultan kept his word to the Portuguese south. On the first of Shaʿbān the thirteen towns of al-Gharb were made a ṭāʾifa, with its seat at [Ukshūnuba](place:ukshunuba), under [Rodrigo of Silves](person:rodrigo-of-silves). He was a Portuguese lord who had taken the shahāda and a soldier of real ability. Gharnāṭa now had three ṭāʾifas, and no overlord at all.

*Indeed, We have given you a clear conquest.*
