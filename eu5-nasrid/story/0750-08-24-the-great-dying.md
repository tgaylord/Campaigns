The peace brought no peace from the pestilence. Through the summer and autumn of the year 750 it came back stronger than before, as a flood that has drawn back comes again over the same ground. It settled worst on the lands newly won, on [Qurṭuba](place:qurtuba) and [Ishbīliyya](place:ishbiliyya) and the towns of the great river. Their walls held a hundred years of Christian crowding and their people were weak from the war that had passed over them twice. Then it climbed back over the mountains into the old realm, into [Gharnāṭa](place:gharnata) and [Mālaqa](place:malaqa), where the houses of healing were full and the cemeteries were fuller.

By the twenty-fourth of Shaʿbān the dīwān had stopped counting by the week and begun counting by the month. The sickness lay over seventeen of the realm's towns. It had killed two hundred and ten thousand of its people, where a year before the toll had been nine thousand. Each month nearly six thousand more were lost. Of the seven hundred and fifty-four thousand who had been the sultan's subjects on the day of the Peace of Qurṭuba, six hundred and twelve thousand remained. In all the known world forty-four million were dead.

These are numbers, and a chronicle can set them down in a line. What they meant is harder to write. It meant the call to prayer given by a boy because the muezzin was dead and his son with him. It meant the *qāḍī*'s court closed for want of witnesses. It meant fields of ripe wheat in the Vega with no one to cut them, and the price of bread rising while the price of land fell. It meant the bells of the Christians in Jayyān tolling from dawn to dusk. It meant that Muslim and Christian and Jew were washed and wrapped by the same hands and laid in trenches side by side, because there was no one left to dig them apart.

The physicians did what they could, and it was not enough.

*Indeed we belong to God, and indeed to Him we will return.*
