On 13 Shaʿbān 822 Daniel died, at fifty. He was the Sephardi who had come to Islam and kept the name he was born with, the first of his people to command a ship of the empire, and in the years after 811 he had taken it further south than any captain of al-Andalus had gone, into the gulf below the bulge of Africa where the coast turns east. He died of a fever at Sanlúcar, where the river of [Ishbīliya](place:ishbiliyya) meets the sea, and the sailors said it was more than most captains who had seen the gulf could hope for, to die in sight of home.

The voyages he had begun went on without him. On 23 Ṣafar 823 the captains of the Green Cape came home with charts of the islands that lie off the westernmost point of Africa, beyond the country of Mālī: their harbours, their springs and the winds that reach them. There was no one on them. Within the month a fleet of settlers sailed for the islands with tools, seed and livestock, to found the first town of the empire in the southern sea. The dīwān's instructions to them were longer than any it had given before. The islands were not to be a fishing post like the others but a land of their own one day, with its own governor and its own people.

The other islands were filling meanwhile. Funchal on Madeira had been a town of the crown since 814, and Maxorata in the Canaries since 818, where the Guanche islanders lived under the Emperor's protection and kept their own gods. On 21 Rajab 823, the same day Balansiyya came into the crown, the settlers of the Azores founded their first town, Angra, on the island of Terceira, thirty months after they had landed. It had a thousand people. Most were Portuguese, Castilian and Leonese peasants from the north, Christians who had taken the Emperor's offer of land in the middle of the Ocean, and they named their church before they had finished their houses. The dīwān did not object. The Emperor said that every town he founded in the Ocean was a town the king of Qashtāla would never found there, and that the faith of its farmers could wait for the madrasa.

*Your Lord is the one who drives the ship for you through the sea that you may seek of His bounty. Indeed, He is ever Merciful to you.*
