On the fifteenth of Ṣafar in the year 749 the thing the merchants had spoken of two years before came ashore. No chronicle records the name of the ship that carried it. It came in the spring with the trade from the east, as the sultan had said it would, while the host was far away on the plains of [Qurṭuba](place:qurtuba). Within days the physicians of the new houses of healing were sending word up to the Alhambra: the swellings, the fever, the black blood. The *ṭāʿūn* had come to al-Andalus.

The sultan's answer was the hardest one. He ordered the infected separated from the healthy. Whole streets were closed with chains, the sick were carried out of the cities to camps beyond the walls, and their houses were sealed with their goods inside. The physicians said it was the only way to slow the sickness, and they were right. But the burghers, whose shops were shut, and the common people, whose sons and mothers were carried off to the camps, cursed the order and the men who enforced it. Some of the *ʿulamāʾ* said that to flee a plague, or to drive others from it, was to doubt the decree of Allāh. Others answered with the Prophet's own words, peace be upon him: if you hear of a plague in a land, do not enter it; and if it breaks out in a land where you are, do not leave it.

When the sultan came back from the frontier in the weeks that followed, he went to the house of healing in [Gharnāṭa](place:gharnata) against the pleading of his physicians. He walked its wards with a cloth soaked in vinegar over his mouth, looked at the swellings as a man who had dressed wounds on the field, and asked each physician what he lacked. What they asked for, the treasury sent.

In the farther villages, where the sultan's officers rode seldom, the people found a different remedy. Elders gathered and named those they held guilty. Sometimes it was a Jew who had sold medicines, sometimes a widow who lived alone, sometimes a stranger who had come up from the coast the month before. Then they drove them out, or worse. The government sent orders to stop it and the orders came too late. It is the oldest sin of frightened men to seek a hand to punish when the hand that strikes is Allāh's.

*Wherever you may be, death will overtake you, even if you should be within towers of lofty construction.* The faithful knew it as a verse. Now they learned it as a season.
