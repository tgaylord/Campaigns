After Antaqīra the war belonged to [Khālid](person:khalid-ibn-yahya). On the twentieth of Rabīʿ al-Thānī 771 he fell on a Marīnid landing at [Abdara](place:abdara) and left none of its nine hundred and seventy alive. In Jumādā al-Ūlā the Egyptians came down from Jarghal and joined him before [Mālaqa](place:malaqa). It was a strange army: Andalusīs, men of Balansiyya, mercenaries from every shore of the sea, and the soldiers of the Mamlūk sultan in their quilted coats. When seven hundred and fifty Marīnids were foolish enough to attack it, they were destroyed without the loss of a single man. In Shaʿbān the Marīnid general Muṣliḥ al-Dīn tried again at Mālaqa with three thousand three hundred and left two thousand four hundred and forty of them under its walls.

In Ramaḍān the Egyptians wanted a victory of their own. With the men of Balansiyya beside them, five thousand six hundred and fifty Mamlūks attacked ʿUthmān at [Sharīsh](place:sharish) on the third of the month. They were thrown back, and three thousand one hundred and ten of them were left in the vineyards. Six days later Khālid came to Sharīsh with seven thousand two hundred and sixty. ʿUthmān lost eighteen hundred and seventy men and Sharīsh, and the Egyptians were avenged. In Dhū al-Qaʿda a last Marīnid army of three thousand three hundred and eighty landed in al-Gharb, hoping to find Gharnāṭa's newest ṭāʾifa undefended. Khālid marched the length of the south to meet it at [Bāja](place:baja), and two thousand two hundred and sixty of the invaders never saw the sea again.

By the summer Fās was finished, though it did not yet admit it. One more battle was needed. On the nineteenth of Ṣafar 772 two thousand eight hundred and ninety Moroccans met eight thousand three hundred and seventy Granadans at Mālaqa, where so much of the war had been fought. They lost a thousand and forty men, and we lost a hundred and fifty-four. That same day the envoys of Abū ʿInān sealed the peace.

Fās gave up the whole northern shore. [Runda](place:runda) and [Marballa](place:marballa) came back to the house of Naṣr, and so did the nine towns of the so-called Kingdom of Sevilla, which ceased to exist: [Qādis](place:qadis), Sharīsh, [Labla](place:labla), Shalūqa, Shidhūna, Arkush, [Jabal Ṭāriq](place:jabal-tariq), [Ṭarīf](place:tarifa) and [al-Jazīra al-Khaḍrāʾ](place:al-jazira-al-khadra). That was eighty-four thousand souls, many of them already Muslims after five years under Fās. The Marīnids would pay reparations and swore to keep the sea free of their corsairs. With its ṭāʾifas, Gharnāṭa held every one of the seventy-two towns of the Strait's three lands, both of its shores. The price was written in the dīwān's registers: some thirteen thousand of the sultan's own men dead in two years of war, and twenty-three thousand of the enemy.

Two days later, on the twenty-first of Ṣafar, the chancellor read a reckoning to the court. The crown of Qashtāla had stopped sending demands. Fās had tried twice to cross and failed both times. The throne in Gharnāṭa had the same occupant it had begun the war with. For a generation every court of Christendom and the Maghrib had expected the house of Naṣr to fall, and it had outlasted them all. The sultan answered in three words, and the scribes wrote them at the head of the register: *Gharnāṭa stands free.* Every town of the old kingdom and the valley of the great river was made a part of the realm for good, the government of the realm was confirmed for a hundred years, and the ṭāʾifas were drawn closer to the throne.

So the scale that had hung between Qashtāla and Fās for twenty-four years came to rest, and it came to rest in Gharnāṭa. Al-Ḥājj Yūsuf began this book as the tributary of one and the prey of the other. His son ended it as the master of the Strait. What the house of Naṣr does with that freedom belongs to another book.

Here ends the Book of the Balance.
