This note covers three entries: the pilgrimage and the Shaykh al-Islām, the turn to the sea, and Barshalūna and the hegemony.

**What the save of 10 October 1435 shows.**
- The seven arsenals are complete.
- The fleet is 50 caravels and 8 of the mod's Andalusī war galleys.
- There's a new naval supplies guild at Cáceres, and a sea exploration is being prepared at Seville.
- Hipalan in the Canaries is a new crown town. The game's Canary province lists it beside Aguere and Tedote, and the chronicle places it at Ipalán on La Gomera.
- The realm's land-versus-naval value moved from −26.4 (July 1432) to −10.2. Negative means land, so the realm moved toward the sea, as Trent describes. The entry shows that shift as the court's and the cities' talk turning to the sea.

**The Emperor and the court, from the same save.**
- The Emperor's admiral role ended on 19 March 1433.
- His hajji modifier dates from 23 November 1433, which the entry takes as his return from the pilgrimage of Dhū al-Ḥijja 836.
- His Ṣūfī modifier from 27 September 1434 confirms the Ṣūfī Shaykh al-Islām.
- He has been regent of Burghush since 21 January 1431.
- The deaths come from the same save. The game gives Abū Ḥammū's death as a suicide; the chronicle tells the court's version.

**From Trent's account, with no save yet.** The annexation of Catalonia (1 June 1436), the explorers (1 May 1436), the Age of Discovery and the Cultural Hegemon title (1 January 1437), and the New World institution appearing in Ishbīliya (screenshot of 1 March 1437).

**Invented colour.**
- *Pilgrimage entry:* the fever of the chest, the sea route and Jyral receiving the Emperor, the news of Temür reaching the pilgrims, the welcome at the river, the Ṣūfīs at ʿArafa, the ʿulamāʾ's expectations, and the brotherhoods' prayers.
- *Sea entry:* the galleys' look, the Cáceres guild's river roads, the Emperor's patronage of masters, poets and young nobles, the pilots and merchants, the sermon at Mālaqa, and the sherbet.
- *Hegemony entry:* the scholars' stocktaking, the books, gardens and masons abroad, the tax-collector, the Emperor's reading habits, and the life of the city in the New World paragraph (its pilots, funduqs, shares and scholars).

**The admiral prince entry (save of 16 January 1438).** Yūsuf's birth, education, traits and three wives come from the save, and their fathers identify them. Amie is Mansa Tiramakhan's daughter. Fāṭima is the daughter of Yūsuf ibn Ismāʿīl of Banbalūna. Muʾmina is the daughter of Muṣliḥ al-Dīn, a Naṣrid courtier. The admiralty is Trent's account; the save doesn't list the role yet. Also from the save:
- the land-versus-naval value crossed to +1.1;
- protected harbours are under construction in five Catalan ports;
- Burghush's regency ended on 11 June 1437;
- Bernarda died on 6 November 1437.

Invented colour: the Emperor's lessons at sea, his reason for the appointment, Amie's journey and dowry, and the court reading the marriages as a map.

**The charter of Majrīṭ (8 April 1438).** This is Trent's account, with no save yet. The Emperor's words are Trent's. The Land Rights terms come from Trent's screenshot of the privilege. Invented colour: Majrīṭ's trades, the burghers' journey, the northern umarāʾ's stake in the market, the clerks' name for the stipend, and the princes' winter of reckoning.

**The scholar Emperor (15 July 1439).** The Scholar trait and the state of the realm come from Trent's screenshot: stability −6, Majrīṭ's city rights still in progress, and the crown appeasing the estates. That stability fell because of the land-rights concession is an inference from Trent's account, not something the game states. Invented colour: what the Emperor read and whom he questioned, his seat at the back of the lectures, his sayings, and the comparison with the risings of 807.

**The trade war (early June 1440).** The Catalan privileges complaint, the Genoese embargo under Angevin pressure, and the Andalusī embargo of Angevin merchants are Trent's account, with no save yet. The markets' ranks come from Trent's screenshot of 1 March 1437. Invented colour: the Catalan houses' consuls and courts, the dīwān's reasoning, London leaning on Genoa "all spring", Prince Yūsuf enforcing the embargo, the merchants' arguments, the Venetians, and the monthly accounting.

**The Jamāʿa of 846 (1 June 1442).** The reform, the burghers' seat and the two privileges are from Trent's account and screenshots. The satisfaction changes on the privilege tooltips are previews taken before each grant. The Emperor's line about city walls echoes the Urban Development Grants text. Invented colour: the Emperor's reading of Rome and Baghdad, his words to the princes, the estates' particular complaints, and the princes' private reaction.

**The princes' mending and the Golden Century (save of 11 June 1442; screenshots of 7 August 1442).**
- The save shows the nobles' Land Rights gone and the two burgher privileges in place. It has the National Assemblies advance, but the reform doesn't yet appear among the implemented reforms.
- Also in the save: estate satisfaction (burghers 74, umarāʾ 32, commoners 34, ʿulamāʾ 44, dhimmī 45), legitimacy 65, and the land-versus-naval value at +26.3.
- The princes' rallying of the commoners and their promise to the ʿulamāʾ are Trent's account.
- The Golden Century wording follows the game's event text.
- The Thalassocracy decision was open on 7 August, but the screenshot doesn't show whether it was taken.
- Invented colour: the princes' ride to the villages, the ʿulamāʾ's grumbling, the shipyards' speed, the readings in the mosques, the secretaries' warning (it paraphrases the Grand Alliance rules), and the Emperor's reply.
- The Jamāʿa entry moved from Book VI into Book VIII, the Golden Century, which opens with it.

**The Jamāʿa of 847 (26 June 1443).** This is Trent's account: a noble agenda to expand colonization, the burghers' support, and Fernando Po as the target. Invented colour: the lords' motives, the vote before noon, the island's description beyond its mountain and people, and the princes' and the Emperor's reactions.
