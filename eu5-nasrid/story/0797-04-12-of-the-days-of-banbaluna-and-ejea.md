The peace of 778 had been made for fifteen years, and the Christians spent the last of them afraid. In Jumādā al-Ākhira 795 Qashtāla, Portugal and the little mountain lordship of Andorra swore a league against al-Andalus. The court at [Ishbīliya](place:ishbiliyya) took it as a compliment. Its spies had been inside Qashtāla for two years. By New Year's Day of 1394 the dīwān had its claim ready, and the ʿulamāʾ gave it its name: the Reconquest of Iberia. Five days later, King Fernando of Qashtāla died at fifty-nine, and his son [Pedro](person:pedro-of-castile), twenty-three, stubborn and fond of his wine, inherited the war his father had seen coming.

On 1 Jumādā al-Ūlā 796 the Emperor [Saʿd](person:saad-ibn-yusuf) declared it. The four ṭāʾifas marched with him, and so did the Moors of Fās with their vassals and the Mamlūks of Miṣr with theirs. With Qashtāla stood Portugal, Andorra, and the Pope in Rome, with his cities of Siena and Faenza.

The sea war went both ways. In Shaʿbān the Moroccan fleet, seventy-two sail, caught the Pope's squadron at the mouths of the Rhône and sank all fourteen of its galleys. Far out on the Ocean, though, a Castilian squadron of only eleven ships hunted the Catalan fleet down three times: off Galicia, at the mouth of the Duero, and in the bay of Setúbal. Of the thirty-three ships Catalonia had sent out, three came home. The Castilians lost none.

On land, the Christians struck first and struck the weakest. In Shawwāl Qashtāla's great host, twenty-four thousand men, came down past Sigüenza onto the small armies of the ṭāʾifas. Aragon's fell back in time. Valencia's did not, and it died there to the last man, three thousand nine hundred and fifty-eight of them, for a hundred and forty Castilians. In the same week the Portuguese came down into the Alentejo and destroyed both armies of the Ṭāʾifa of the Algarve, at Setúbal and at Montemor.

The Emperor had seventeen thousand six hundred men and no wish to meet twenty-four thousand on their own terms. He followed them north instead. Through Shawwāl and Dhū al-Qaʿda his host shadowed the Castilians up the Aragonese border, from [Qalʿat Ayyūb](place:qalat-ayyub) toward the Ebro and Tudela, with the Catalans and Aragonese on their other flank, waiting for the Castilians to make a mistake. On 16 Dhū al-Ḥijja they made it. They had chased an Aragonese army of eight thousand nine hundred into the hills of Navarre, and at [Banbalūna](place:banbaluna) they found the Emperor across their road. Their pursuit was cut off and their host beaten, two thousand four hundred and forty-one of theirs lost for one thousand four hundred and fifty of ours, and the Castilians went back the way they had come in haste.

They came south again with the cold weather, still twenty-one thousand seven hundred strong, under the best general left to Qashtāla. [Agustín Arce](person:agustin-arce) was Grand Master of the Order of Alcántara, fifty years old, a soldier since boyhood. On 10 Ṣafar 797 the Emperor caught him on the plain of the Cinco Villas at Ejea de los Caballeros. It was ground of Arce's choosing, and the officers told the Emperor so. He attacked anyway. For nine days the Grand Master held, and the knights of Alcántara were all that the chronicles of their order would later claim. On the ninth day they broke. Ten thousand eight hundred Castilians were dead or scattered, against four thousand six hundred and sixty-four of ours. That one field moved the war further than the whole of the summer before it.

What was left of Arce's host fled west into Old Castile, ten thousand eight hundred and eighty-six men with no fight left in them. On 12 Rabīʿ al-Thānī, at Peñafiel on the Duero, eight hundred and ninety-seven horsemen of the Ṭāʾifa of Aragon found them. King [Alfons](person:alfons-de-borja)'s riders cut down or scattered ten thousand five hundred and sixty-four men, and did not lose one of their own. Qashtāla had no army left in the field.

Only in Tuscany did the war go badly for the faithful. There the men of Barqa and a Mamlūk detachment, sent to keep the Pope busy at home, were beaten at Grosseto, Piombino and Montalcino by the Sienese. The Pope stayed busy, though, and he sent nothing more to Qashtāla.

*If Allāh should aid you, no one can overcome you.*
