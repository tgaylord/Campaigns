The return of al-Andalus did not happen in the mosques first. It happened in the kitchens and the markets, in the songs mothers sang over cradles and the words men used to haggle over wool. The tongue came back before the faith.

The first town to turn was Baena, in the hills south of Qurṭuba, some time in the years of the Marīnid war. The dīwān's surveyors found that its people now counted themselves Andalusīs and not Castilians. They spoke the Arabic of the south, kept its feasts and wore its clothes, and they still went to Mass on Sundays. Over the next three years the surveyors found the same thing again and again. [al-Quṣayr](place:al-qusayr) turned in 764, [Balma](place:balma) in 765, and [Istijja](place:istijja), [Qusanṭīna](place:qusantina) and Fuenteovejuna in 766. Towns that had been Castilian for a century became Andalusī within a single generation, and stayed Christian. The jurists did not know what to make of them. Some said they were halfway to Islam. Others said they were Mozarabs again, the Christians of al-Andalus who had lived under the caliphs and spoken their tongue, as their ancestors had been before Qashtāla came. Both may be true.

Faith came more slowly, and it needed help. In Shaʿbān 765 the Jamāʿa restored the jizya and began a new campaign of preaching. The jizya is a hard teacher but a patient one. A Christian of [Ishbīliyya](place:ishbiliyya) who paid it every year was reminded every year what conversion would save him. When the campaign began, thirteen in every hundred souls of Ishbīliyya were Muslims. A year later it was twenty-six. In [Jayyān](place:jayyan), where scarcely one soul in fifty had prayed toward Makka when the war began, three in ten did so by the time it ended.

Then [Qurṭuba](place:qurtuba) turned. In the spring of 767, in the weeks of the peace, the surveyors counted more Andalusīs in the city than Castilians, for the first time since Ferdinand of Qashtāla took it a hundred and thirty years before. In 749 there had been thirty-four thousand Castilians to six thousand Andalusīs. Now there were nineteen thousand five hundred Andalusīs to sixteen thousand three hundred Castilians. Six months later, on the twenty-sixth of Muḥarram 768, the count of the faithful was finished. Eighteen thousand two hundred Muslims, eighteen thousand Christians and three thousand four hundred Jews lived in the city of the caliphs. By the narrowest of margins, Qurṭuba was a Muslim city again.

The sultan was told in Gharnāṭa. He is said to have asked only one question: whether the great mosque was still a cathedral. It was. He said that he could wait. The mosque had waited a hundred and thirty years, and the people of Qurṭuba would give it back themselves when they were ready. That same year the treasury, filled at the peace, paid for another Great Madrasa, this one at Ishbīliyya.

*There is no compulsion in religion. The right way has become distinct from the wrong.*
