For thirty years the army of al-Andalus had been a small thing. It was a few thousand horse and foot kept for the frontier, with the ṭāʾifas' levies behind it and the fleet in front. Since the crusade the crown's gold had gone into harbours, bridges and the trades of the towns, and the dīwān had been content to let the sea do the realm's fighting. The war for Balarm changed that. Corsica had fallen and Sardinia was nearly gone, and still the war could not end until Balarm was taken. The allies had broken themselves against its walls four times. The Ḥafṣid sultan no longer asked for ships. He asked for men.

In the middle of Ramaḍān 853 the two princes put it to the dīwān. [Ismāʿīl](person:ismail-ibn-muhammad-ibn-ismail) made the case. Genoa's mainland could be raided forever without ending anything, he said. Sicily was the war, and the war should be finished where it had begun. [Aḥmad](person:ahmad-ibn-muhammad-ibn-ismail) made the reckoning. A landing on Sicily needed an army that would not melt away when the harvest called the levies home. The ṭāʾifas' levies were not that army, and neither were the few thousand the crown already kept. The Emperor [Muḥammad](person:muhammad-ibn-ismail-ibn-saad), who loved the fleet and had never much loved the army, gave them what they asked.

The princes raised four thousand men of the thughūr, the frontier marches. They were men from the old border towns whose fathers had held the line against Qashtāla, and they were paid soldiers from the first day to the last. It was the first true enlargement of the army in a generation. The muster-masters at [Ishbīliya](place:ishbiliyya) had to find out what such a thing cost, and the treasury found out with them.

The court had news from the east that winter, and it made the choice seem wiser. In the lands of the Hungarian crown the king of Bohemia had been at war for two years over the succession, with Poland and half the princes of the Empire behind him. By the summer of 853 his armies held all of eastern Hungary. Nābulī had gone to war on Hungary's side. The princes of Italy were looking north and east, not south.

On 2 Muḥarram 854 eight thousand seven hundred men of al-Andalus came ashore at Saraqūsa, on the eastern shore of Sicily. There were no levies among them. Every man was a soldier by trade, and the oldest captains said there had not been such an army under the Naṣrid banner since the wars of the Istirdād. Balarm lay on the other side of the island.
