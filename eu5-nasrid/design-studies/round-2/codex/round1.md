# Designer A, round one

## 1. The Codex, with Canon Tables

**Answers:** 1 (the spine) and 3 (one entry), with 4 (the returning reader) along the way.

**What the reader sees.** One continuous book in date order. Each entry is a leaf, and its address is its Hijri date (772.02.21), which never changes as the book grows. The table of contents is a set of **canon tables**, like the arcaded concordances that open Gospel books. Years run down the page and each book that is open at the time gets its own column, so you can see the threads braid: Balance and Pestilence side by side from 1346 to 1352, or the Call, the Balance and the Marches closing on three days running in Ṣafar 772. The 1379–1394 gap shows up as a narrow empty row. Read across the rows for the whole book in order, or down a column for one thread.

On the entry's own page each book has its own rubric colour and a large opening initial. A running head gives book and reign on one side, Hijri and Western year on the other. The outer margin carries **glosses** at a name's first mention, such as "Faraj, 20, named heir this year, the sultan's elder brother". The age is worked out for that date. Sources sit at the foot as an apparatus, marked as read, estimated or invented. The verse is set as a colophon. Below it are two **catchwords**, the opening words of the next leaf in the book and the next leaf in this thread. A ribbon remembers where you stopped. A colophon page at the back logs each session's new leaves.

**Material:** entries, books, people and places. Missing: a one-line gloss for each person, which can be drafted from people.json, and the out-of-character notes.

**Reusable:** all of it. The palette, the Hijri addresses and the verse are specific to this campaign.

**Effort:** medium. **Biggest risk:** the margins and the wide tables on a phone. There the glosses become tap-markers that open a sheet, and the tables fold by decade.

## 2. Annals and Chronicle: the facing-page edition

**Answers:** 2 (narrative and reference together), and gaps and provenance. It also offers a second spine, by year.

**What the reader sees.** Medieval houses kept two kinds of record. *Annals* were a bare line for every year, and a year could stand empty ("1385. Nothing."). A *chronicle* told the story. This edition puts both on facing pages, the way a bilingual edition prints the original opposite its translation. The left-hand page is the chronicle entry, untouched. The right-hand page is the annals for the span the entry covers, set small and plain in ledger type. They are the dated events from its sources, each marked as read from a save, as Trent's account, or as invented colour. They sit beside the state of the realm at the nearest save (ruler, towns, souls, treasury) and the out-of-character note. The events follow the reader's paragraph as it scrolls. Names on the right are the plain ones (Seville, Castile), which gives the switch between name forms a natural home.

The annals also run on their own as the year spine. There is one line for every year from 1337 onwards, including the empty ones. Each line says whether that year was read from a save, told from memory (most of the 1380s), or is silent, and it lists the leaves that hang from it.

**Who it favours:** Trent. Friends get a reading copy with the right-hand page closed.

**Material:** events (762, with sources), checkpoints, realm names. Missing: links from paragraphs to events, which is the hard part. Matching by date gets most of them, and the writer could add anchors from now on.

**Reusable:** wholly. **Effort:** medium-high. **Biggest risk:** the right-hand page turning into a dashboard that competes with the prose. It has to stay quiet type, never charts.
