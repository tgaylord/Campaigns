# Designer B, round one: time as the spine

What the material says about time, before the ideas (all checked against `brief2/`):

- **Entries are moments that reach back.** Each has one date, but its `sources` often go years behind it. *Of the Encircling Sea, and Fifteen Years of Building* (1395) cites events from 1376 on; *The Second Survey of the Kingdoms* (1420) cites 1370. The death of Ismāʿīl (1429) deliberately calls back to the death of Saʿd (1405).
- **The quiet is real, and it gets filled later.** 46 of the 107 Western years have no entry of their own. There's no save between Dec 1379 and Mar 1394. The 1380s are told afterwards, by entries of 1395 and even 1428.
- **The books are threads, not volumes.** Four are open at once today (the Call, the Encircling Sea, the Franks, the Golden Century). Entries in different books share dates: on 18 Oct 1346 the Houses of Healing sit beside the Summons of the Marīnids, and between 13 and 15 Sep 1370 three books hand over in three days.
- **The calendar carries meaning.** The chronicle writes Hijri first, and its filenames are Hijri dates (`0832-09-25` is 25 Ramaḍān 832). It reads death at the start and at the end of Ramaḍān as a pattern. The span 737–847 AH is 111 Hijri years against 107 Western ones.

---

## 1. The Annals (*al-Ḥawliyyāt*): "Then came the year 832"

**Answers 1 and 5.** The great Arabic histories, al-Ṭabarī's and Ibn al-Athīr's, are kept by year. Each year opens with a rubric, *then came the year…*, and everything in that year follows it: a war, a plague, a birth. Here the Hijri year becomes the spine.

**What the reader sees:** one continuous scroll of years. Each year has a red rubric in Hijri, with the Western years under it (switchable), and a margin note giving the reign and regnal year ("the seventh year of Saʿd"). Entries sit under their year, each marked with its book's colour and Arabic title. When entries from different books share a date, they're set side by side under the heading *on the same day*. Silent years aren't hidden. A run of them folds into one hatched rubric, "The years 782–795: no entry of their own, told in…", which links forward to the later entries that tell them. Those links are computed from each entry's sources. "Follow a thread" dims the other books without leaving the scroll. Typing a year in either calendar jumps to it. At 400 years, years fold into reigns and reigns into ages, as zoom levels of the same scroll.

**Material:** entry dates, source dates and ruler changes, plus a small Hijri (tabular) converter. Nothing essential is missing.

**Carries over:** the annal form plus a calendar plug-in (Hijri here; era names, Anno Mundi or regnal years elsewhere). Specific to this campaign: the rubric wording.

**Effort:** modest. **Risk:** strict chronology breaks up each book's arc, so a "read this book straight through" order is needed alongside it. Friends are favoured here.

## 2. The Hourglass: one date the whole site obeys

**Answers 1, 2 and 5; touches 4.** This idea leaves the book as it is and gives the reader a position in time.

**What the reader sees:** a quiet rail along the page edge (on a phone, a strip that pulls up). It runs from 1337 to now, to scale, with the reigns as bands and the books as hairlines that run side by side where they overlap. It has a tick for every entry and every one of the 72 saves, and hatching over the gaps between saves. A bead marks where you are. Drag it, and the page opens at the nearest entry. A retrospective entry draws its reach on the rail.

**Everything is "as of" the bead.** Tap Faraj in 1370 and his card shows what was known then. The realm panel quotes the last save before that date, says how old it is ("two days earlier", or "fourteen years: the record is silent here"), and flags figures that are estimated. For friends, the future is veiled: no death dates and no later titles. Trent lifts the veil and sees his last-read mark and what's new since his last session. This isn't round one's scrubbed map, which was a lens on data. It's a reading contract.

**Material:** almost everything is already dated: births and deaths, the ranges when places were held, wars, checkpoints with an `estimated` flag. Missing: a "first mentioned" date per person, which can be derived from the entries.

**Carries over:** nearly all of it. **Effort:** medium to high, because every reference view has to take a date. **Risk:** the rail turns into chrome, or a stale save gets passed off as current.
